Research note for the GT Éducation, 18 November 2026. Question: is the master's degree losing value for young graduates relative to the bachelor's (2023-2026), and what does that mean for France, where 29.1 % of 25-34 year olds hold at least a master's level degree, against 19.8 % in the UK, 16.7 % in Germany and 13 % in the US (OECD, cited by J.-C. Simon, Les Echos, 9 Nov 2026; used as given, see `research/article-smicardisation-formation-2026-11.md`).

**Bottom line.** Levanon's claim is about master's holders compared with **their own past**, not with bachelor's holders. In the US, young master's holders still have lower unemployment than bachelor's-only holders, but the gap closed in 2025. In France, bac+5 graduates still clearly outperform bac+3/4 on employment, unemployment, pay and stable jobs at three years (Céreq, Génération 2021). The French weak points are elsewhere: the 2024-2025 fall in junior cadre hiring, and a high and rising share of university master's graduates who are not cadres.

## 1. What it shows

### Verified (primary source read)

1. **Levanon: master's unemployment is high for master's holders, not higher than for bachelor's holders.** Gad Levanon (Burning Glass Institute), "The Masters Degree Problem", *Labor Matters*, 13 April 2026: unemployment of master's holders (MA, MS, MBA, MEd) under 35 sits at the **77th percentile** of its own 2003-2026 history (24-month moving average), against the **22nd percentile** for professional and doctorate holders (MD, JD, PhD). The post gives no rates in percent, no bachelor's comparison and no named data source (the NY Post / WSJ coverage says BLS data). Mechanisms he proposes: (a) supply glut, notably online MBAs and data or analytics MS programs; (b) a master's is "a signal rather than a license", and "signals lose value when everyone has one"; (c) sector exposure, as technology-driven weakness in finance, consulting and tech hits master's holders, who are concentrated there. https://gadlevanon.substack.com/p/the-masters-degree-problem . The LinkedIn version (title "Think young college grads have it bad? Look at those with master's degrees") returned HTTP 403 and **was not read**; I assume, without being able to verify, that it is the same analysis.
2. **US, ages 25-34, CPS (BLS via FRED, not seasonally adjusted, annual averages computed by me).** Unemployment of master's holders: 1.9 % in 2022, 2.1 % in 2023, 2.5 % in 2024, 3.2 % in 2025, 2.9 % in Jan-Oct 2026. Bachelor's-only: 2.5 %, 2.8 %, 3.1 %, 3.3 %, 3.5 %. Master's holders are still below bachelor's holders every year; the gap fell from 0.8 point in 2023 to 0.1 point in 2025, then reopened to 0.6 point in 2026. Over Nov 2025-Oct 2026: 3.0 % against 3.4 %. Series CGMD2534 and CGBD2534, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CGMD2534 . Monthly values are noisy (for example 1.9 % against 3.3 % in October 2026).
3. **US recent graduates in general (NY Fed, 22-27, bachelor's or higher).** Unemployment 5.2 % in 2026 Q3, its lowest in more than a year; underemployment 42.5 %, up in the quarter. The NY Fed series does not split master's from bachelor's. https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market
4. **France, three years after leaving education (Céreq, Génération 2021, surveyed 2024).** Master's (university, level 7) against bac+3/4: employment **87 % vs 78 %**, unemployment **9 % vs 13 %**, median first income **1,810 € vs 1,530 €** net per month, stable jobs (CDI, civil servant, self-employed) **71 % vs 67 %**. Engineering and business schools: 90 % employment, 7 % unemployment, 2,130 €. Overall the hierarchy by diploma level holds, and the Céreq writes that "l'hypothèse d'une inflation des diplômes ... n'est donc pas privilégiée". But on **déclassement** (normative: a bac+5 is déclassé if not cadre or business owner; a bac+3/4 if not cadre or profession intermédiaire), "la situation relative des diplômés de master ... est souvent moins favorable que celle des diplômés de bac+3/4 ou même de niveau bac". Overall déclassement rose from 26 % (Génération 2004) to 32 % (Génération 2021), mostly among short-cycle tertiary graduates; it has been falling for a decade among bac+5 and above. Ilardi, Lercari, Merlin, Céreq Bref 487-488, July 2026, https://www.cereq.fr/sites/default/files/2026-07/Bref_487-488_OK.pdf
5. **France, 12 months after graduating (MESR-SIES, InserSup, graduates under 30 not continuing studies).** Salaried employment in France at 12 months, 2024 graduates: **licence professionnelle 79.8 %, master (excluding teaching) 70.4 %, licence générale 61.9 %**; master down 0.8 point on 2023. Net monthly full-time-equivalent pay of 2022 graduates (interquartile range): master 1,850 to 2,610 €, licence pro 1,710 to 2,170 €, licence générale 1,530 to 1,950 €. Note Flash SIES n° 33, December 2025, https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2025-12/nf-sies-2025-33--38991.pdf . Caveat: the vocational licence pro beats the master on early salaried employment, but not on pay; the comparison excludes self-employment and work abroad.
6. **France, the 2024-2025 shock to bac+5 hiring (Apec).** Salaried employment at 12 months of bac+5 graduates fell from 74 % (class of 2022) to **72 % (class of 2023)**; business schools 66 % (-4 points), IT 75 % (-4), engineers 77 %, letters-languages-arts 59 %. Junior cadre hiring fell 19 % in 2024 and was forecast to fall 16 % in 2025, driven by IT services, engineering-R&D, legal, accounting and consulting services. 84 % of the 2024 class found the job search difficult (61 % for 2022); 24 % call their job "alimentaire" (+7 points). Apec attributes the fall to the investment slump and political uncertainty, **not to AI**. Apec, "Jeunes diplômé.es d'un Bac +5", November 2025 (InserSup, plus a survey of 1,595 graduates, June 2025), https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/sites/default/files/2025-11/Apec-Jeunes-diplom%C3%A9s-Bac%2B5-une-insertion-plus-difficile.pdf

## 2. How it applies to France

The French version of the argument (Simon: "smicardisation et surqualification", a "machine à frustration") runs: France produces twice the US share of master's holders, into an economy whose wage structure is compressed towards the SMIC (Rexecode: +8 points in 30 years of private employees paid below 80 % of the average wage), so many master's holders end up in jobs below their expectations.

**What holds.**
- The master's is less often a ticket to cadre status: only about half of university master's graduates start as cadres, and their normative déclassement compares badly with bac+3/4 (Céreq). Levanon's "signal, not license" point fits the French university master's well, as against engineering and business schools.
- France had its own 2024-2025 shock, concentrated in the same sectors Levanon names (IT, consulting, finance and accounting services): junior cadre hiring -19 % then -16 %, business-school graduates down 4 points (Apec).
- The vocational licence pro does better than the master on early salaried employment (79.8 % vs 70.4 % at 12 months), which supports the case for shorter, applied tracks.

**What does not hold.**
- **The French bac+5 still clearly outperforms the bac+3/4** on every Céreq indicator at three years: +9 points of employment, -4 points of unemployment, +280 € of median first income. The Céreq explicitly rejects diploma inflation for bac+5 and finds déclassement falling at that level.
- Even in the US, young master's holders are not doing worse than bachelor's holders in levels; they are doing worse than they used to.
- The master's median first income (1,810 €) is well above the SMIC; the smicardisation problem bites hardest on non-graduates (whose median first income is below one full-time SMIC, Céreq) and on short-cycle graduates, whose déclassement rose most.

**Honest framing for the notes:** the problem is not "too many masters" in general, but too many master's programs that do not lead to cadre jobs, and too little information about which ones do. That supports note 3's proposal to publish outcomes by programme, rather than a call to cut master's places.
