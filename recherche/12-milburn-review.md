## 3. The debate around it

**Government** [P]: it agrees with the "core principles" and supports the guarantee and local delivery. It is creating a Youth Opportunities Unit, will publish a White Paper in the new year and legislate afterwards, and will weigh the report with the Timms review. It cites the existing Youth Guarantee (£2.5bn; "nearly one million" young people; up to 500,000 opportunities) and £3.5bn for disability employment support. **Not addressed:** the National Insurance cut, the Youth Allowance and PIP replacement, the Talking Therapies fast-track, the AI Observatory and the 270,000 target.

**Parties**
- **Conservatives** (Helen Whately, shadow DWP secretary): back tighter rules for anxiety, ADHD and depression claims, but oppose "a massive expansion in the size and responsibilities of the state". She notes that savings are only £3.6bn against the party's pledge of up to £36bn of welfare cuts. [Press] City AM, 17 Nov 2026: https://www.cityam.com/massive-expansion-of-the-state-milburn-report-sows-tory-and-labour-division-on-welfare/
- **Reform UK**: Danny Kruger is reported as "encouraged" by stricter benefits for young people. No formal statement found. [Press, UNVERIFIED as to date and context]
- **Liberal Democrats**: Ed Davey called it "bold" and Caroline Voaden welcomed universal work experience. [Press; the quote may relate to the interim report, UNVERIFIED]

**Think tanks**
- **Resolution Foundation** (Ruth Curtice, 17 Nov) [P]: right scale of ambition. UK 15-24 NEET 13.6% in 2025, against 4.8% in the Netherlands. Its concerns: the under-25 rate of under £80 a week (20% below the 25+ rate) is inadequate. The reform creates losers among people unable to work. Most young PIP recipients (almost two thirds) are already in work or education, so PIP reform should not be designed around NEETs. https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/press-releases/government-must-match-milburns-ambition-to-properly-invest-in-young-people/
- **IFS**: page blocked (403). The search summary says the IFS found the package appropriately system-wide, needing priority at the 2027 Spending Review, and the modelled employment effects hard to judge on the detail published. [Press, UNVERIFIED] https://ifs.org.uk/articles/milburn-review-young-people-and-work-ifs-response
- **Youth Futures Foundation** (Sarah Yong, 17 Nov) [P]: welcomes the 5% "lodestar". Its concerns are local capacity and inactivity that is still rising. https://youthfuturesfoundation.org/news/youth-futures-foundation-responds-to-young-people-and-work-report/
- **Bright Blue** (Ryan Shorthouse): "bigger and bossier government". Its fix would be cheaper hiring plus tighter access to health benefits. [Press, City AM]
- **Learning and Work Institute, IPPR, Policy Exchange, CSJ**: no response to the final report found on 18 Nov. Before the report, IPPR projected NEETs rising from one in eight to one in six by 2029, and L&W set a goal of under 10% NEET with nobody NEET for more than a year. [Press]

**Evaluation evidence on earlier schemes**
- **Kickstart (2020-22)**, DWP impact assessment, 7 Oct 2024 [P]: 163,000 placements. For every 100 participants, 11 more were in unsubsidised work at 24 months than comparable non-participants, and 3 more were off UC. Return per £1: £1.18 at 2 years and £3.15 at 5 years (all perspectives); £0.27 and £0.49 to the Exchequer. Larger effects for people with weaker prospects. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/kickstart-scheme-a-quantitative-impact-assessment/executive-summary-kickstart-scheme-a-quantitative-impact-assessment . The NAO found weak assurance on additionality and job quality. [Press]
- **Future Jobs Fund (2009-10)**: +11 percentage points in unsubsidised work and -7 points on benefits at 2 years (DWP, Nov 2012, via NIESR). [Press; DWP PDF not opened] Milburn (p.161) states that the FJF and Kickstart effects were "remarkably similar". [P]
- **Youth Contract wage incentive (2012-14)**: only employer surveys found. 19% of employers said they created an extra vacancy (DWP RR828, self-reported). No causal impact estimate found, so **UNVERIFIED**.

**French NEET figures**
- 15-29: **13.3%** in Q3 2026, stable on the quarter and +0.4 point on the year. Youth unemployment for 15-24s is 21.0% (Insee, Informations rapides n° 270, 10 Nov 2026). [P] https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/9118603
- 15-24: **10.5%** in 2023, against 9.2% for the EU. For 25-29s it is 16.3% (EU 15.1%) (Insee, Formations et emploi, 12 Feb 2025). [P] https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/8305542?sommaire=8306008
- EU 15-29: 11.0% in 2025; Netherlands 5.3% (Eurostat, 28 May 2026). [P]
- **Comparability warning.** UK ONS uses ages 16-24 (13.0%) and France reports 15-29 (13.3%). On the same 15-24 band, the UK (13.6%, OECD 2025) is well above France (10.5%, 2023). Do not put 13.0% and 13.3% side by side as if they were comparable.

**Read-across.** Milburn converges on what France already has in law: a guarantee with an allowance (CEJ), a duty to 18, and a local youth network. What France lacks, and what Milburn proposes: (a) a guaranteed paid job as a backstop; (b) a school-level **destination measure** and a work-readiness standard, both inspected; (c) a body tracking **entry-level jobs** under AI by sector and place; (d) mandatory early-warning (RONI) tools in school.
