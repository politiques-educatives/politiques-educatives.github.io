## Executive summary (ranked by strength of evidence)
1. **The fall is international, not French-specific (strong, descriptive).** PISA 2025: France's reading score fell 18 points since 2022 after 19 points in 2018-2022; the OECD average fell 14. France tracks the OECD (DEPP NI 26.40). Any explanation must work across countries.
2. **Covid explains part of the adolescent fall, not the primary one (moderate).** Learning losses persisted (d = -0.14), but are larger in maths than reading. The 2026 CE1/CE2 pupils were not yet at school in 2020.
3. **Less reading for pleasure, more screens (moderate, correlational).** Ages 16-19: over 5 h of screens a day vs 14 min of reading (CNL 2026). Fits a French-only, adolescent-heavy decline. No causal estimate.
4. **Distraction in class (moderate, correlational).** PISA 2022: 15 points lower in maths where peers' devices distract.
5. **Phone bans (mixed causal evidence).** Gains in England 2016 and Norway (girls); null in Sweden 2020, England 2025, US 2026.
6. **Lower test effort (weak).** French 15-year-olds report the lowest effort in the OECD bar Luxembourg; weakly tied to scores.
7. **Mental health (weak link to scores).** Trends coincide; no study linking them to French test scores was found.
8. **Teacher shortages, hours, composition, test changes (weak or untested).** Plausible locally; no direct evidence on the 2026 fall.

## 1. What the DEPP says
- **2026 results (SECONDARY, ministry page via search and economiematin.fr, 18 Nov 2026).** Seconde GT: 22.1% in the two weakest French groups vs 12.4% in 2021; maths 28.1% vs 27.6% in 2021. French "taux de maîtrise satisfaisante" -1.6 pts on 2025, in all sectors. CP reading aloud 68.3% vs 74.9% in 2021; CE1 dictation 69.9% vs 73% in 2023; 4e mean score 243 vs 250 in 2023. https://www.education.gouv.fr/evaluations-nationales-de-septembre-2026-506811 ; https://www.economiematin.fr/evaluations-nationales-baisse-francais
- **No causal attribution.** Press coverage reports the DEPP director, Magda Tomasini, only pointing to new collège curricula still being rolled out (SECONDARY, economiematin.fr). The two 2025 notes (NI 26.21, 26.22, June 2026) are purely descriptive: no mention of Covid, screens or reading time (VERIFIED, text searched).
  - NI 26.22: seconde GT French fell between 2024 and 2025 "et reviennent au niveau de la rentrée 2019"; 20.2% in groups 1-2 (+1.4 pts on 2024); seconde pro French down since 2021; social gaps in French widened by 2 points on 2024 (VERIFIED). https://www.education.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/document/depp-ni-2026-22test-de-positionement-secondepdf-517325.pdf
  - NI 26.21: 6e still above 2017 in both subjects; 4e French mean down vs 2023 (first year of the 4e test), maths stable (VERIFIED). https://www.cafepedagogique.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/depp-ni-2026-21evals-2dpdf-517319.pdf
- **The only DEPP causal hint is Covid, and it is cautious.** The PISA note says the broad fall since Covid "soulève la question d'éventuels effets durables de cette période" (VERIFIED, NI 26.40, Sept 2026). It links the PISA fall to the seconde test fall since 2021, with a caveat that the two do not measure the same skills.
- **Cohort arithmetic (our calculation, check with DEPP).** In spring 2020, 4e 2026 pupils were in CP, seconde 2026 pupils in CE2, 6e 2026 in moyenne section. CE1/CE2 2026 pupils were not yet at school. Covid school closures therefore cannot explain the 2026 primary falls. They fit the 4e cohort, which lost part of CP, but 6e (stable) undercuts a simple "Covid generation" story.
- **CSEN / Cnesco:** no 2025-26 CSEN or Cnesco statement on causes of the reading fall found (UNVERIFIED gap). The États généraux de la lecture pour la jeunesse (2025) report that parents blame screens and young people cite uninteresting books (SECONDARY, culture.gouv.fr).

## 2. OECD PISA
- **PISA 2025 (VERIFIED, DEPP NI 26.40, Sept 2026):** https://www.education.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/document/depp-ni-2026-40pisa-mathspdf-520231.pdf
  - Reading: France 456 vs OECD 461; -18 pts 2022-25, -19 pts 2018-22; OECD -14 pts on 2022. Only 8 OECD countries did not fall.
  - Below level 2 in reading: 33% (27% in 2022, 21% in 2018, 15% in 2000); OECD 30%. Boys -23 pts, girls -11 in three years; 39% of boys below level 2 vs 26% of girls.
  - Maths: 458 vs OECD 463; -16 pts on 2022 (OECD about -9), after -21 pts 2018-22.
  - Screens: 4.9 h/day on devices on school days, of which 3.0 h leisure, vs 3.4 h leisure OECD. High-scoring Estonia (4.6 h) and Poland (4.9 h) report more. **So France's fall is not explained by unusually high screen time.**
  - Effort: 6.4/10 vs 7.2 OECD; 7.0 in 2022 and 7.2 in 2018. Effort is "faiblement corrélé" with scores (450 pts for effort 4-6 vs 447 for 9-10).
- **PISA 2022 Vol. II (VERIFIED, PDF copy of OECD 2023 report):** about 30% of OECD students get distracted using devices in most or every maths lesson; 25% by others' devices. Students distracted by peers' devices in at least some lessons score **15 points lower** in maths, after socio-economic controls. Students using devices for leisure at school up to 1 h/day did better than heavier users ("Goldilocks"). In schools with a phone ban, 29% still use a smartphone several times a day; bans are associated with less distraction. https://csi.gov.cz/CSICR/media/Prilohy/2024_p%c5%99%c3%adlohy/Dokumenty/PISA_2022_Vol_II.pdf
- **France vs OECD distraction:** 58% of French students distracted by own devices in at least some maths lessons vs 65% OECD (SECONDARY, search summary of OECD *Students, digital devices and success*, 2024; oecd.org blocked). https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/students-digital-devices-and-success_9e4c0624-en.html

## 3. Reading for pleasure
- **PIRLS 2021 (CM1):** France 514, stable vs 511 in 2016, below the EU average of 527 (SECONDARY, DEPP NI 23.21 via search). https://www.education.gouv.fr/depp/pirls-2021-la-france-stabilise-ses-resultats-contrairement-aux-autres-pays-europeens-majoritairement-452301
- **CNL / Ipsos BVA 2026 (7-19 year olds, n = 1,500, April 2026):** 18 min/day of leisure reading vs 3 h 01 on screens; 8 min less reading than in 2016. Ages 16-19: over 5 h/day of screens vs 14 min of reading; about one third (mostly boys) do not read for leisure (SECONDARY, AFP via linfodurable.fr, 14 Apr 2026). https://www.linfodurable.fr/les-jeunes-lisent-toujours-moins-surtout-ladolescence-selon-une-etude-55889
- **CNL 2024:** 19 min/day reading, -4 min vs 2022; 3 h 11 on screens (SECONDARY). https://www.ipsos.com/fr-fr/comprendre-la-lecture-chez-les-jeunes-francais-tendances-et-defis-en-2024
- Read: the reading decline is steepest at 16-19, which matches the steepest test decline (seconde, especially pro) and the boy-girl gap in PISA. This is consistent, not causal.

## 4. Smartphones, social media and learning
- **Causal or quasi-causal ban studies:**
  - England, Beland and Murphy (Labour Economics, 2016, vol. 41): scores +6.41% of a standard deviation after bans, larger for low achievers (SECONDARY; working paper figure; published figure not checked). https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:labeco:v:41:y:2016:i:c:p:61-76
  - Norway, Abrahamsson (NHH DP 1/2024; reported in JHR online 4 Mar 2026): event study; girls' GPA up, less bullying for both sexes, fewer psychological care visits for girls; larger for low-SES girls (VERIFIED abstract, no numbers; JHR publication SECONDARY). https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/hhsnhheco/2024_5f001.htm
  - Sweden, Kessel et al. (IFN WP 1288, 2020): no effect on performance (SECONDARY). https://www.ifn.se/media/fcxcel1o/wp1288.pdf
  - England, Goodyear et al. (Lancet Regional Health Europe, 2025): 30 schools, 1,227 pupils, cross-sectional; restrictive policies not associated with better wellbeing, sleep or attainment; pupils compensated outside school (SECONDARY). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11850730/
  - US, Florida (Figlio et al., 2025): under 1 percentile point gain in year two; US pouch study (Dee et al., NBER, May 2026): close to zero at three years (SECONDARY, Hechinger/FutureEd, 28 May 2026). https://www.future-ed.org/what-the-latest-research-on-school-cellphone-bans-reveals-and-what-it-doesnt/
  - Netherlands (ban Jan 2024): about three quarters of 317 secondary schools report better concentration; self-reported survey, not an impact study (SECONDARY). https://www.yahoo.com/news/study-finds-smartphone-bans-dutch-160724853.html
- **France, « Portable en pause »:** trial with over 32,000 pupils in 2024-25; about 80% of participating collèges favourable (ministry answer to the Senate, Jan 2026, SECONDARY); SNPDEN survey (Sept 2025): 9 collèges in 10 not applying it (SECONDARY). **No impact evaluation on learning found.** https://www.senat.fr/questions/base/2025/qSEQ25120857S.html
- **Debate.** Haidt argues for a causal smartphone effect; Odgers (Nature, 2024 review) and Orben and Meier (Nature Reviews Psychology, 2024) find mixed, mostly small associations (SECONDARY). https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-024-00307-y
- **Commission « Enfants et écrans » (April 2024):** see our digest, `research/09-sante-mentale-inclusion/2026-11-17-digest-rapports-ecrans-ia-elysee.md`, section 1 (social media a risk factor "quand il existe une vulnérabilité préexistante"; proposal 17 on phones at collège).

## 5. Mental health and learning
- **Link to scores:** no study found estimating how much of the French score decline is due to adolescent mental health (UNVERIFIED gap). The Norway ban study is the closest evidence that one lever (phones) moves both grades and mental-health care use for girls.
- **Covid learning loss (VERIFIED abstract):** Betthäuser, Bach-Mortensen and Engzell (Nature Human Behaviour, 2023), 42 studies, 15 countries: deficit d = -0.14 (95% CI -0.17 to -0.10), arose early, persists; larger for low-SES pupils and **larger in maths than reading**. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01506-4 . This pattern does not fit France 2026, where maths rises and French falls.
- **PISA 2025 family support** fell (49% say parents take an interest in what they learn vs 63% in 2022), which the DEPP reads as a return to pre-Covid norms (VERIFIED, NI 26.40).

## 6. Other explanations
| Explanation | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Teacher shortages | CAPES lettres modernes 87.7% of posts filled in 2025 (88.8% in 2024); about 96% in 2026 (SECONDARY, ministry and press). Contractuels about 8% of teachers in 2024, +2 pts since 2015 (SECONDARY, DEPP NI 26.37); 11.1% in public secondary (Senate, 2024). No study links this to the 2026 fall. https://www.education.gouv.fr/concours-enseignants-de-la-session-2025-463026 | Plausible, untested |
| Fewer French hours | No source checked today (UNVERIFIED) | Not assessable |
| Composition | Seconde: 76% GT, 24% pro in 2025 (VERIFIED, NI 26.22). Falls appear in all sectors and both voies; DEPP shows widening social gaps, not a changing intake | Weak |
| Test changes | 4e test only since 2023, CE2/CM1 recent, so trends are short; DEPP scales are anchored over time (NI 26.21 methodology, not checked) | Weak, but limits trend claims |
| Primary maths policy | Maths rising in primary, plausibly from the maths plan and new curricula (UNVERIFIED) | Not assessed |

**Best supported:** an international post-2018 decline, partly Covid, with a French reading-specific layer concentrated in adolescents and boys that matches the fall in leisure reading. Screens and phones are a plausible channel with correlational support; school phone bans have mixed causal effects.

## Link to our notes (French, ready to paste)

**(a) Note santé mentale (`notes/sante-mentale/`)**
- La baisse touche surtout les adolescents : en seconde générale et technologique, 22,1 % des élèves sont dans les groupes les plus faibles en français en 2026, contre 12,4 % en 2021 et 18,7 % en 2019 (DEPP, test de positionnement de seconde 2026, vérifié).
- Elle n'est pas propre à la France : à 15 ans, le score en compréhension de l'écrit a perdu 18 points depuis 2022, contre 14 points en moyenne dans l'OCDE (DEPP, NI 26.40, septembre 2026).
- En Norvège, l'interdiction du smartphone au collège a amélioré les notes des filles et réduit leurs consultations pour troubles psychologiques (Abrahamsson, 2024), mais d'autres études ne trouvent aucun effet (Goodyear et al., 2025) : « portable en pause » doit donc être évalué, ce qui n'a pas été fait (à vérifier).

**(b) Note IA et jeunes, condition « De bonnes bases pour tous »**
- Les jeunes de 16 à 19 ans passent plus de cinq heures par jour sur les écrans et 14 minutes à lire (CNL, avril 2026) : c'est à cet âge que le français recule le plus.
- Un tiers des élèves de 15 ans est désormais en difficulté de lecture, contre 15 % en 2000 (PISA 2025, DEPP NI 26.40) ; ouvrir l'IA en accès libre à des élèves qui ne lisent pas encore couramment risque d'aggraver cet écart.
