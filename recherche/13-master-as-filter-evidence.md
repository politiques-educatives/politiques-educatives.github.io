## 1. Degree inflation and the requirement gap (US, UK)

- **SECONDARY.** Fuller and Raman, *Dismissed by Degrees* (HBS, Accenture, Grads of Life, Oct 2017): 26 million 2015 postings plus a survey of 600 business and HR leaders. **67 %** of production supervisor postings asked for a bachelor's degree, against **16 %** of people already in the job; 6.2 million middle-skill jobs "at risk of degree inflation". HBS PDF returned 404: https://www.hbs.edu/managing-the-future-of-work/Documents/dismissed-by-degrees.pdf ; summary https://pw.hks.harvard.edu/post/dismissed-by-degrees
- **SECONDARY.** Burning Glass Institute and HBS, *The Emerging Degree Reset* (Feb 2022, 51 million postings, 2017 vs 2019-Mar 2021): degree requirements fell materially in **46 %** of middle-skill and **31 %** of high-skill occupations; production supervisor postings asking for a BA fell to 33 % by 2020. https://www.bollettinoadapt.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/TheEmergingDegreeReset22.02Final.pdf
- **Outcomes are weak.** Sigelman, Fuller, Martin, *Skills-Based Hiring: The Long Road from Pronouncements to Practice* (BGI and HBS, Feb 2024; authors and date [P], figures **SECONDARY**): removing a degree requirement raised non-degree hiring in those roles by **3.5 points** on average, about **97,000** extra hires a year out of 77 million (fewer than 1 in 700); most of the change came from 37 % of firms; at "leader" firms non-degree hires had retention 10 points higher than graduates. https://burningglassinstitute.org/research/skills-based-hiring-2024 (PDF download blocked)
- **US states.** [P] National Governors Association, 11 Feb 2025: "over half of U.S. state governments" have skills-based hiring policies, including dropping degree requirements; non-degree postings grew faster in those states (postings, not hires). https://www.nga.org/news/press-releases/new-report-growing-number-of-states-drop-degree-requirements/ . Exact count **SECONDARY**: 20 to 27 states depending on definition (14 by executive order, Oct 2024). Federal government: not checked.
- **UK.** [P] Indeed Hiring Lab (L. Feist, 29 Aug 2024): only **14 %** of UK postings mention any education requirement (about 50 % in the US); **4.6 %** require a bachelor's or higher, **under 1 %** a master's or doctorate; 52 % of sectors ask for degrees less than six years earlier. https://hiringlab.indeed.com/uk/blog/2024/08/29/job-postings-formal-education-requirements/ . Civil Service Fast Stream still asks for a 2:2 but counts degree apprenticeships (**SECONDARY**, gov.uk page 403). Milburn final report (17 Nov 2026) [P, via `research/12-milburn-review/`]: apprenticeships as top skills priority, about 270,000 under-25 starts a year by 2036; 11 % of Civil Service entry staff aged 16-24 by 2032. **NOT FOUND**: any Milburn recommendation to drop degree requirements.

## 2. France: what junior jobs ask for, and over-qualification

- **NOT FOUND:** a study of the share of French junior ("jeune diplômé", "débutant") offers requiring bac+5 or prior experience (Apec, France Travail, Indeed, HelloWork). Apec defines "débutant" as under one year of experience. Lack of experience is the top obstacle cited by bac+5 graduates (**79 %**, Apec Nov 2025, **SECONDARY**, apec.fr 403).
- [P, CSO Ireland reproducing OECD PIAAC 2023, Table 3.1; workers 25-65, not self-employed]: **France 19 % over-qualified** against **OECD 23 %**; under-qualified 12 % vs 9 %; well matched 69 % vs 67 %. Over-skilled 25 % vs 26 %. France is *less* over-qualified than average. https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-piaac/programmefortheinternationalassessmentofadultcompetenciespiaac2023-skillsuseandsocialoutcomes/skillsmismatch
- Céreq Génération 2021 (in companion file): normative déclassement 32 % overall, falling for bac+5; but master's graduates compare badly with bac+3/4 on it.

## 3. Public service

- [P] Teacher recruitment (devenirenseignant.gouv.fr): **since the 2026 session** CRPE, Capes, Capeps, CPE, Capet and CAPLP are open from the third year of licence. Laureates then do a two-year master: M1 as élève fonctionnaire, about **1,400 € net/month**; M2 as fonctionnaire stagiaire, about **1,800 €**, half-time in class; 4-year service commitment. https://www.devenirenseignant.gouv.fr/reforme-du-recrutement-et-de-la-formation-initiale-des-professeurs-mieux-former-pour-mieux-faire-1516 . Decree of 19 April 2025 (n° 2025-352?) **UNVERIFIED**. Note: the master is kept, but selection moves to bac+3 and the master becomes paid training.
- Attaché d'administration (IRA, first category A grade): external concours requires a licence (bac+3) (**SECONDARY**, emploipublic.fr). "More than 80 % of external laureates hold a master or grande école diploma" (**UNVERIFIED**, carrieres-publiques.com).
- [P] Mon Master 2025 (SIES Note Flash 32, Dec 2025): **258,300** candidates (+10 %), **68 %** received an offer (71 % in 2024), 56 % accepted one. By origin: L3 générale **82 %**, BUT 57 %, licence pro **48 %**. https://www.enseignementsup-recherche.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2025-12/nf-sies-2025-32-38737.pdf

## 4. Signalling

- [P, abstract] Jaeger and Page, "Degrees Matter", *Review of Economics and Statistics* 78(4), 1996: using actual degree receipt (CPS 1991-92) rather than years of schooling substantially raises the estimated diploma ("sheepskin") effects for high school and college. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:tpr:restat:v:78:y:1996:i:4:p:733-40 . Magnitudes (+24.5 % college) **SECONDARY**. Hungerford and Solon (1987, *ReStat*): citation only, content **UNVERIFIED**.
- [P] **French quasi-sheepskin evidence.** Insee Première n° 2109 (E. Pouliquen, June 2026), cohort entering 6e in 2007, aged 26-27 in 2023, comparing graduates with those who reached the final year and failed or left. Master: employment 90 % vs 87 % (**not significant**); net pay 2,240 € vs 2,130 €, **+4 %** after controls; **cadre 56 % vs 42 %, +15 points** after controls (social origin, sex, past achievement). Licence: cadre or profession intermédiaire +17 points. So the master *certificate* barely changes pay or employment, but strongly gates access to cadre jobs. https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/9003866
- Caveat: this is consistent with signalling or credential rules, but final-year learning and residual selection are not excluded. **NOT FOUND**: a French estimate of the master vs licence return net of selection.

## 5. Social unfairness

- [P] RERS 2025, table 7.16-3 (French students at universities, 2024-2025, unknown origin excluded): children of cadres **31.1 % in licence, 42.1 % in master**, 41.6 % in doctorate; children of ouvriers **10.1 %, 6.3 %**, 3.9 %; employés 20.8 %, 14.3 %, 10.0 %. All students: 36 % cadres, 9 % ouvriers. https://www.education.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/rers2025-chapitre-7-441732.pdf
- **NOT FOUND:** Mon Master offer rates by bursary status or social origin (2024 and 2025 notes give none). "Nearly one in three" 2023 registrants were boursiers (**SECONDARY**, letudiant.fr).
- SIES (Nov 2025): licence in 3-4 years for 48.8 % of very favoured vs 32 % of less favoured students (**SECONDARY**, snippet).

## 6. Cost

- [P] RERS 2025, table 10.05-2 (2023, provisional, 2023 prices): **12,250 € per university student per year**; all higher education 13,060 €; STS 16,730 €; CPGE 18,560 €. Two master's years ≈ **24,500 €** of spending (my calculation; master-specific cost not published). https://www.education.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/document/rers-2025-chapitre-10-522891.pdf
- Living costs (**SECONDARY**): UNEF Aug 2025, about **1,226 €/month** (≈ 29,400 € over 24 months, my calculation); FAGE 2025, 3,227 € start-of-year cost for a non-bursary licence student; fees 178 € (licence) vs 254 € (master).
- Forgone earnings (my calculation, crude): 24 months at the Céreq bac+3/4 median first income (1,530 € net) ≈ 36,700 €, less if unemployed. The master's +280 €/month median premium (1,810 € vs 1,530 €) takes about 11 years to repay that, ignoring pay growth and the master's better employment rate.

## 7. Employer surveys

- [P] Lumina Foundation and Gallup, 2,500 US hiring decision-makers, 5-18 Aug 2026 (opt-in, unweighted): **80 %** prefer a candidate with a degree (31 % strongly), and the preference holds even where the role does not require one. https://news.gallup.com/poll/715601/employers-confidence-higher-education-undaunted.aspx
- **SECONDARY:** Cengage 2023, 50 % of 1,000 US employers require a degree for entry jobs (62 % in 2022); *Dismissed by Degrees* survey: degree holders cost 11-30 % more without matching productivity (**UNVERIFIED**).
- **NOT FOUND:** any French survey (public or private employers) on whether entry jobs truly need the degree level demanded. The proposed poll would fill a real gap.

## Verdict per claim

| Claim | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Gap between required and needed level | **Partly** | Strong US evidence (67 % vs 16 %); in France only indirect (IRA licence rule vs mostly-master laureates, unverified); no French ad study |
| (b) Master mainly a filter/signal | **Partly** | Insee: certificate adds +15 points cadre access but only +4 % pay; yet master still beats bac+3/4 on jobs and pay; human capital not ruled out |
| (c) Unfair to poorer students | **Supported** (access) | Cadres 31 % → 42 %, ouvriers 10 % → 6 %; selection by bursary status not measured |
| (d) Costly for young people | **Supported** | ≈ 29,000 € living costs + forgone earnings, ≈ 24,500 € public money; partly repaid by the premium |
| (e) Wastes talent | **Weak / contrary** | France 19 % over-qualified vs OECD 23 %; bac+5 déclassement falling; only Céreq's master vs bac+3/4 comparison and Apec "jobs alimentaires" support it |

## Five French sentences for a campaign note

1. « À l'université, les enfants de cadres représentent 31 % des étudiants de licence mais 42 % des étudiants de master ; les enfants d'ouvriers passent de 10 % à 6 % (RERS 2025, année 2024-2025). »
2. « Une année d'université coûte 12 250 euros par étudiant (RERS 2025) : deux ans de master, c'est environ 24 500 euros d'argent public, plus environ 1 200 euros par mois de frais de vie pour l'étudiant (UNEF 2025, à vérifier). »
3. « L'État a montré la voie : depuis 2026, on passe le concours de professeur dès la licence, puis le master est payé environ 1 400 puis 1 800 euros nets par mois (ministère de l'Éducation nationale). »
4. « Le diplôme de master ouvre surtout une porte : à 26-27 ans, l'obtenir plutôt qu'échouer en dernière année augmente de 15 points la probabilité d'être cadre, mais le salaire de 4 % seulement (Insee, juin 2026). »
5. « Aux États-Unis, 67 % des offres de chef d'équipe de production exigeaient une licence, alors que 16 % des titulaires du poste en avaient une (Harvard Business School, 2017, à vérifier) ; en France, personne n'a mesuré cet écart, d'où notre consultation des employeurs. »
