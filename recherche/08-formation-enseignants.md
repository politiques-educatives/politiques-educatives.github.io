Research for the proposal: (1) initial teacher training (ITT) through recreated
"écoles normales" outside the university; (2) continuing professional
development (CPD) inside service time (ORS, HSE), targeted on the weakest
teachers identified by inspection.

**Status (2026-10-26): partly verified.** The shared web-search budget ran out
after two searches; ccomptes.fr, education.gouv.fr, OECD, EEF, IFS, SAGE and
UChicago returned 403 or 404. Everything below marked **VERIFIED** was read in
the primary source or a direct report of it. Everything marked **UNVERIFIED**
comes from memory or a secondary source and must be checked before use.

**What this means for the proposal:** the 2025-2026 reform moved the concours
and paid the students, but it left training inside the university (INSPÉ). An
"école normale" outside the university would go further than current law.

### Diagnoses of INSPÉ and earlier systems

- **Cour des comptes, référé June 2018 (ESPE):** two-year master cost about
  **1.1 bn €**; « Ni le ministère, ni les universités [...] ne connaissent et
  ne suivent le coût »; some tracks had **< 10 students**; M1 risked becoming
  exam cramming; recommended admissibility at end of licence (Acteurs publics, above). VERIFIED.
- **Cour des comptes, report of February 2023** (« La formation initiale et le
  recrutement des enseignants »): framework « demeure instable » since 2010;
  recommended at least **one week** of compulsory training for contract
  teachers, and professional licences for primary (linfodurable 01/02/2023).
  VERIFIED via press report; the report itself was not fetched. No figure on
  trainer profiles was found in the coverage.
- **IGÉSR 2022**, « La formation initiale des professeurs des écoles en France :
  une évolution nécessaire à l'aune des standards européens »: cites TALIS
  2018, « seuls 34 % des enseignants français ont dit s'être sentis "bien
  préparés" ou "très bien préparés" »; recommendation 15 gives the MEN a
  « pouvoir de prescription sur les maquettes de formation des INSPÉ » (as
  quoted by SNESUP, Feb 2024). VERIFIED as quoted; the original was not fetched.
- **Assemblée nationale, Le Vigouroux and Genevard, January 2024** (reading):
  « L'une des causes principales de la faiblesse des résultats des élèves
  français en lecture tient à la faiblesse de la formation de leurs
  enseignants » (p. 40, quoted by SNESUP). VERIFIED as quoted.
- **Sénat:** the culture committee gave senators **M. Brisson and A. Billon** a
  mission on « la formation et la place des professeurs dans le système
  éducatif » on **7 February 2024** (SNESUP). The report's findings were not
  fetched (UNVERIFIED).
- **Trainers who are not teachers / share of practice:** the 2019 reform's
  rule that **at least one third** of INSPÉ teaching is given by practitioners
  in schools is from memory: **UNVERIFIED**, check the arrêté of 28 May 2019 on
  the MEEF framework.

### Criticism of the reform and of the "école normale" idea

- SNESUP-FSU (Feb 2024): the reforms rest on an « attaque directe des INSPÉ »
  without any assessment of the previous reforms. Moving the concours to L3
  will « accroître mécaniquement le vivier » but is not enough without
  higher pay. VERIFIED (union position).
- Counter-figures from the same source: **92 %** of MEEF graduates are in work
  at 12 or 18 months, against **75 %** for all masters (SIES note 2023-32);
  only **~10 %** of serving teachers came through a MEEF master (Réseau des
  INSPÉ estimate); « près de 50 % » of teachers began without seeing a pupil
  during their training; contract teachers were **22 % in 2022** (Réseau des
  INSPÉ). VERIFIED as quoted; the 22 % base (of new hires? of all teachers?) is
  unclear.
- **Implication:** the INSPÉ defence ("few teachers come through us") also
  cuts the other way. The weak link is the large number of entrants (contract
  teachers, career changers) who skip structured ITT altogether.

## C. Evidence on CPD design and on targeting

| Study | Design | Key result | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fletcher-Wood and Zuccollo (EPI for Wellcome, 19 Feb 2020) ([link](https://epi.org.uk/publications-and-research/effects-high-quality-professional-development/)) | Review of **52 RCTs** of teacher CPD | Effect **0.09 SD** on pupil outcomes, close to the **0.11 SD** gain from a teacher with 10 years' experience against a new graduate; tutoring 0.28 but costlier; CPD « may be a cost-effective intervention » | VERIFIED. The **35 h/year** entitlement figure is not on the summary page: UNVERIFIED (check full report) |
| Kraft, Blazar and Hogan (2018), *RER* 88(4), 547-588 | Meta-analysis of **60** causal coaching studies | Instruction **+0.49 SD**, achievement **+0.18 SD**; effects smaller in larger programmes | 0.2 SD on achievement VERIFIED via CAE Note 93; 60 studies, 0.49 and the scale finding UNVERIFIED (SAGE 403) |
| Visscher et al. (2025), *Educational Research Review* 49, 100742 | Meta-analysis of **128** high-quality studies | Effects need classroom anchoring, individual support, enough duration and focus on precise skills | VERIFIED via CAE Note 93 |
| Grenet and Landais (2025), CAE Note 84 | Synthesis | Intensive good-quality CPD **~+0.12 SD** | VERIFIED via CAE Note 93 |
| EEF (2021), *Effective Professional Development* guidance; Sims et al. (2021) systematic review and meta-analysis | Meta-analysis of PD RCTs | **14 mechanisms** in four groups: build knowledge, motivate teachers, develop techniques, embed practice. Programmes with mechanisms in all four groups do better | UNVERIFIED (EEF 403). Effect sizes of Sims et al. not obtained |
| Taylor and Tyler (2012), *AER* 102(7), 3628-51 ([link](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.102.7.3628)) | Cincinnati evaluation system (TES), mid-career teachers with value-added | Teachers « more productive in post-evaluation years », with « the largest improvements among teachers performing relatively poorly ex-ante » | VERIFIED (abstract). Magnitude (~0.11 SD in maths) UNVERIFIED |
| Papay, Taylor, Tyler and Laski (2020), *AEJ: Economic Policy* 12(1), 359-388; NBER w21986 ([link](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21986)) | RCT, Tennessee (with Jackson-Madison County Schools): low performers paired with a high performer in the same school, on skills flagged in their own evaluations | Students of treated low performers **+0.12 SD**; gains persisted, possibly grew, the following year; mechanism: learning skills from the partner | VERIFIED |
| Burgess, Rawal and Taylor (2021), *Journal of Labor Economics* (EEF "Teacher Observation" trial) | RCT, ~80 English secondary schools, peer observation | Gains for pupils of both observed and observing teachers, ~0.07 SD | UNVERIFIED (publisher 403) |

**For targeting:** the two studies that tie CPD to evaluation (Taylor and
Tyler; Papay et al.) both find the **largest gains among low performers**, and
both use inspection or evaluation data to pick who gets support. This is the
closest evidence for the "inspection identifies, CPD follows" design. Caveat:
both are US settings with value-added or rubric scores; French inspection gives
far less frequent and less standardised ratings.

### Evidence comparing routes

- **IFS (Allen, Belfield, Greaves, Sharp, Walker, 2016)**, "The longer-term
  costs and benefits of different initial teacher training routes": retention
  differs by route; Teach First has lower retention after the two-year
  commitment; school-led routes are not clearly cheaper once school costs are
  counted. **UNVERIFIED** (IFS 403), figures not obtained.
- **Allen and Allnutt (2017)**, *BERJ*, Teach First impact on GCSE results:
  small positive school-level gains, about one grade in one of a pupil's best
  eight subjects. **UNVERIFIED**.
- **NFER** retention by route: not obtained. **UNVERIFIED**.
- No source found that compares **outside-university vs inside-university
  ITT** on pupil outcomes. In the evidence I know of, route effects on pupil
  outcomes are small next to differences between teachers within a route
  (UNVERIFIED, general finding of the US routes literature).
