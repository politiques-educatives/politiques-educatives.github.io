## Bottom line on the claim being tested

> Claim: "There is no solid RCT evidence that ability grouping is ineffective, and the unions opposed the 2024 groupes de besoins citing research."

| Part of claim | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| No solid RCT evidence that grouping is *ineffective* | **Largely true for between-class grouping; but there is no solid RCT evidence that it is effective in rich countries either.** | The best rich-country RCT (EEF/UCL "Best Practice in Setting", 127 schools) found ~0 effect, but fidelity collapsed, so it tests nothing cleanly. The one large clean RCT of tracking (Kenya, Duflo-Dupas-Kremer 2011) found **+0.14 SD, including for low achievers**. Meta-analyses of mostly non-experimental studies find between-class grouping ≈ 0 (g = 0.04 to 0.06). |
| "Research shows grouping hurts weak pupils" (union line) | **Overstated.** | EEF reads a *small* negative for low attainers, on evidence it rates weak; Steenbergen-Hu et al. (2016) find effects "did not differ" by ability level; Kenya RCT finds gains for low achievers. Harm to the weakest is plausible *when low groups get lower expectations and a thinner curriculum* — the French IGÉSR observed exactly that in 2024-25. |
| Unions cited research | **True.** | SNES-FSU (April 2024) cited Cnesco (Lafontaine 2017), Dupriez and Draelants (2004), Draelants (2023 interview). But the Cnesco text they quote also says homogeneous groups "peuvent permettre d'améliorer les résultats des élèves en difficulté" under conditions. |
| The courts ruled on evidence | **False.** | Both Conseil d'État decisions turned on **competence** (who signs the text) and, in 2026, a minimal "erreur manifeste d'appréciation" review. No ruling on effectiveness. |
| Strongest evidence in the area | **Tutoring and within-class / flexible regrouping**, not permanent between-class setting. | Nickow et al. (2020): 0.37 SD pooled over 96 RCTs; within-class grouping g = 0.19 to 0.30. |

### IGÉSR interim report (May 2025): what it found
Sample: **39 collèges, 8 académies** (some press: 9), year 2024-25. A wider 1,500-collège evaluation was announced for autumn 2025: **result not found (UNVERIFIED whether published)**. Source: https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/groupes-de-besoins-au-college-un-premier-bilan-negatif
- Most colleges formed **level groups**; some formed mixed groups with smaller numbers; only "une infime minorité" formed groups around specific skills.
- "une quasi-absence de mobilité" between groups; the 10-week return to the reference class was rarely used.
- Pupils with high needs: the measure "n'a clairement pas bénéficié" to them; "risque majeur" of widening gaps before the return to full classes in 4e.
- Groups made up mostly of pupils with **low needs** benefited most.
- Teachers mostly responded to difficulty by **lowering expectations and cutting content**.
- Recommendations: abandon the uniform model (all 6e-5e, full French-maths timetable), more autonomy for heads, train teachers in managing heterogeneity and severe difficulty.
- **Not a causal evaluation**: no control group. **No DEPP impact evaluation found** (UNVERIFIED that none exists).

**Reading**: the IGÉSR describes the textbook failure mode of the literature (fixed groups, low expectations in low groups, no regrouping), not a test of well-run flexible grouping.

## B. Evidence on ability grouping, by design

### (i) Between-class grouping (tracking, streaming, setting)

### (ii) Within-class grouping
- **EEF Toolkit, within-class attainment grouping**: about **+3 months**, evidence rating cut from "moderate" to "limited"; "likely to be beneficial for all learners", "less benefit for lower attaining pupils". Older/other versions: **+2 months**, "very limited", **22 studies**. https://www.tes.com/news/exclusive-mixed-ability-classes-more-effective-sets ; https://evidenceforlearning.org.au/toolkit/within-class-achievement-grouping
- Steenbergen-Hu et al. (2016): **g = 0.19 to 0.30**. Lou et al. (1996): **+0.17** vs no grouping; homogeneous vs heterogeneous small groups **+0.12** (via Hattie summaries).

### (iii) RCTs and quasi-RCTs of regrouping by level

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Duflo, Dupas and Kremer (2011)**, AER 101(5), 1739-1774 | **RCT**, 121 Kenyan schools (61 random assignment vs 60 tracking), grade 1 | Tracking schools **+0.14 SD** after 18 months, **persisting one year after**. "Students at all levels of the distribution benefited"; pupils near the median gained similarly in upper or lower section. Positive direct peer effect, but tracking let teachers "teach to their level". https://www.nber.org/papers/w14475 . Context: classes split thanks to an extra (contract) teacher programme, so both arms had smaller classes (details UNVERIFIED this session). |
| **Teaching at the Right Level** (Banerjee et al. 2016, NBER WP 22746; JEP 2017 31(4), 73-102) | Series of RCTs, India | Regroup pupils by actual level (not grade) for part of the day. **Haryana** (teachers, dedicated hour): language **+0.15 SD**. **Uttar Pradesh** (volunteer "learning camps", 40 days + 10-day summer camps, during school hours): **+0.70 SD**. Training/materials alone: no effect. https://www.nber.org/papers/w22746 ; https://www.aeaweb.org/research/proof-of-concept-scalable-policy-rct-challenges |
| **TaRL Zambia "Catch Up"** (de Barros and Lubozha, J-PAL) | National-scale RCT, >1,100 schools, 182 zones + 4.4m exam scores | Gains in taught literacy and basic maths after 2 years; better leaving exams later. Extra teacher PD **doubled cost, no extra gain**. SD figures **UNVERIFIED**. https://www.povertyactionlab.org/updates/national-study-zambia-finds-focus-teaching-basics-leads-broad-learning-gains-over-time |
| Joplin plan / cross-grade regrouping | Older quasi-experiments | Positive (Steenbergen-Hu: g = 0.26). Dated evidence. |

**Developing-country caveat**: Kenya and India have very wide within-class dispersion and curricula far above most pupils' level, which makes level-matching more valuable than in a French 6e.

### Effects on low achievers and peer effects
- Correlational and quasi-experimental tracking studies: low tracks often lose (EEF, Lafontaine), largely through **curriculum and expectations** (Draelants; IGÉSR 2025 observation).
- Experimental evidence: low achievers **gain** (Kenya), or no differential (Steenbergen-Hu 2016).
- Peer-group engineering can backfire: Carrell, Sacerdote and West (2013, *Econometrica*) placed low-ability US Air Force Academy cadets with high-ability peers and found the low-ability group did **worse**, because students re-sorted socially (**from memory, UNVERIFIED this session**). Booij, Leuven and Oosterbeek (2017, REStud) found tracking at a Dutch university helped low and middle ability students (**UNVERIFIED**).

## C. Small-group tutoring

### Nickow, Oreopoulos and Quan (2020), NBER WP 27476 (read from the PDF)
96 RCTs, 732 estimates. Pooled **0.37 SD**. Published 2024 version: **0.288 SD** (SE 0.029) (https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/promise-tutoring-prek-12-learning-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis-experimental-evidence). https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476

| Moderator (Table 3A) | Effect (SD) | Studies |
|---|---|---|
| Teacher tutors | 0.50 | 18 |
| Paraprofessional tutors | 0.40 | 46 |
| Volunteers | 0.21 | 24 |
| Parents | 0.23 | 11 |
| During school | 0.40 | 78 |
| After school | 0.21 | 17 |
| 1:1 | 0.38 | 67 |
| 1:2 | 0.29 | 14 |
| 1:3 or more | 0.36 | 24 |
| 1-2 days/week | 0.24 | 19 |
| 3 days/week | 0.34 | 33 |
| 4-5 days/week | 0.41 | 46 |
| Grades 6-11 | **0.16** | **8** |
- Authors: ratios above 1:1 "statistically similar" for teachers/paraprofessionals; "little evidence of once-weekly tutoring sessions generating large effect sizes".
- **Relevant for collège**: only 7% of interventions involve grade 6+; secondary effect much smaller (0.16) and thin.

## D. Conditions under which grouping or tutoring works

| # | Condition | Evidence | Teacher time / timetable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Flexible, short-cycle regrouping with frequent reassessment**, around a precise skill, not a fixed "niveau" | Cnesco 2017 ("flexible et réévalué", limited to one sequence); within-class g 0.19-0.30 vs between-class ≈ 0 (Steenbergen-Hu 2016); IGÉSR 2025: "quasi-absence de mobilité" in failed rollout | Yes: assessment and regrouping need common planning slots |
| 2 | **Same curriculum and high expectations in low groups** | Between-class grouping "without changing the curriculum" ≈ 0; harm channel is lowered goals (Draelants 2023; IGÉSR 2025: lowered expectations, cut content) | Partly: needs shared progressions across teachers |
| 3 | **Grouping bundled with extra time, not substituting for class time** | Double-dose algebra +0.15 SD ACT, +8.6 pp college (Cortes et al. 2015); TaRL Haryana dedicated hour +0.15 SD | **Yes: extra hours = ORS / HSE** |
| 4 | **Teach at the pupil's actual level for part of the day only** | TaRL (+0.15 to +0.70 SD); Kenya tracking +0.14 SD | Timetable |
| 5 | **Tutoring during the school day, inside the timetable** | During 0.40 vs after 0.21 SD (Nickow 2020); Saga embedded in schedule; APFEE after-school null | **Yes: requires timetable slots** |
| 6 | **Small groups: 1:1 to 1:3 (≤ 4); 5+ is no longer tutoring** | 1:1 0.38, 1:2 0.29, 1:3+ 0.36 (Nickow); Saga 2:1; APFEE groups of 5 null | Staffing ratio |
| 7 | **Dosage ≥ 3 sessions/week, ≥ 20 h per pupil** | 3 days 0.34, 4-5 days 0.41, 1-2 days 0.24 (Nickow); NTP optimum > 20 h (NFER 2024) | **Yes: hours** |
| 8 | **Trained, paid tutors (teachers or paraprofessionals), not volunteers** | Teacher 0.50, paraprofessional 0.40, volunteer 0.21 (Nickow); NTP school-led beat external partners | Yes: who delivers, under which status |
| 9 | **Aligned timetables across classes ("heures en barrettes")** so groups can be formed across classes | Cnesco 2017 recommendation; IGÉSR 2025: uniform full-timetable model creates organisational strain | **Yes: timetabling** |
| 10 | **Fidelity and school buy-in over scale** | Best Practice in Setting: half the schools dropped out, null; NTP drive "for scale over quality" (Lampl); Zambia: extra PD added cost, no gain | Yes: leadership time, autonomy for heads (IGÉSR rec.) |

**ORS relevance**: conditions 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9 depend directly on teacher service time (common planning hours, extra hours, timetable alignment). Under the current French ORS (18 h of teaching for certifiés, no dedicated planning time; figures not re-sourced here, UNVERIFIED), flexible grouping and in-school tutoring require either recognised concertation hours or annualised service.

## 10-line summary
1. The 2024 arrêté (15 Mar 2024) imposed groupes de besoins in 6e-5e français-maths; the CE annulled it on **competence** only (28 Nov 2024) and said it did not breach the collège unique.
2. A décret + arrêté of 4 Apr 2025 restored it in 6e-5e (4e-3e dropped); the CE upheld them on 27 Feb 2026, again with no ruling on evidence.
3. The arrêté of 10 Mar 2026 made groups **optional** from rentrée 2026 ("accompagnement pédagogique renforcé").
4. IGÉSR (May 2025, 39 collèges, not causal): fixed level groups, near-zero mobility, lowered expectations, no benefit for the weakest. No DEPP impact evaluation found.
5. Unions did cite research (Cnesco 2017, Lafontaine, Dupriez-Draelants), but the same Cnesco work endorses temporary homogeneous groups under conditions.
6. The user's claim holds: there is no solid RCT showing grouping is harmful; but there is no solid rich-country RCT showing between-class setting works either (EEF/UCL trial null, poor fidelity).
7. Meta-analyses: between-class grouping ≈ 0 (g 0.04-0.06), within-class 0.19-0.30, cross-grade 0.26; EEF −1 or 0 months for setting, +3 for within-class.
8. The only big clean tracking RCT (Kenya, 2011) found +0.14 SD for all levels; TaRL in India +0.15 to +0.70 SD.
9. Tutoring is the strongest lever (0.37 SD, 96 RCTs), but weaker in secondary (0.16 SD) and in scaled programmes (NTP ≤ 1 month; French APFEE null).
10. It works when flexible, high-expectation, in-school, 1:1 to 1:3, ≥ 3 sessions/week, with aligned timetables and planning time: mostly a **teacher-time and timetabling** question.
