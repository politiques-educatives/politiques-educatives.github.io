## A. France: how teachers are inspected today

### A1. The PPCR "rendez-vous de carrière" (since 2017-18)

Legal basis: décret n° 2017-786 du 5 mai 2017 and arrêté du 5 mai 2017 (cited in the 2022 ministry guide and the Paris 2025-26 circular: https://paris.snes.edu/IMG/pdf/i-dpe-25-00063_circulaire_rendez-vous_de_carriere_2025-2026.pdf ; https://education.gouv.fr/media/48144/download). Before PPCR, each teacher had a numerical "note pédagogique" set at inspections whose timing was irregular.

| RDV | When (classe normale) | What it decides | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| RDV 1 | 2nd year of the 6th échelon (12 to 24 months of seniority in it) | 1-year acceleration to the 7th échelon for 30% of the eligible group | Paris circular 2025-26; Martinique PPCR presentation 2017 https://site.ac-martinique.fr/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2017/04/PPCR-Pr%c3%a9sentation-pour-enseignants.pdf |
| RDV 2 | 18 to 30 months of seniority in the 8th échelon | 1-year acceleration to the 9th échelon for 30% | same |
| RDV 3 | 2nd year of the 9th échelon | Timing of promotion to the **hors-classe** | same |

- Format: a classroom inspection plus two interviews, one with the inspector and one with the chef d'établissement (secondary), no more than 6 weeks apart; teachers are told at least one month (some circulars say 15 days) in advance (SNES Mayotte https://www.mayotte.snes.edu/IMG/pdf/reunion_ppcr_sur_rv_carriere.pdf ; Besançon note de service https://ash.circo25.ac-besancon.fr/wp-content/uploads/sites/23/2022/10/Note-de-service-n°2-PPCR-2024.2025.pdf).
- Outcome: four-level "appréciation finale" (Excellent / Très satisfaisant / Satisfaisant / À consolider), set by the recteur (or IA-DASEN for primary).
- **The RDV 3 appréciation is kept for all later hors-classe campaigns** ("pérenne"): La Réunion note de service 2023 (https://www.ac-reunion.fr/media/46418/download) uses the RDV 3 appraisal, or the one given in previous hors-classe campaigns.
- Outside the RDV, PPCR provides for "accompagnement" (individual or collective, visits, meetings) but with no set frequency (Martinique 2017; Nancy-Metz https://sites.ac-nancy-metz.fr/dsden54-circo/ientoul/sites/ientoul/IMG/pdf/ppcr.pdf).

### A2. Grid timing: when does the last compulsory inspection happen?

Classe normale, certified teachers (CGT grid 2024, https://www.fnte.cgt.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/Triptyque%20Cat%20A%20Professeur%20Certifi%C3%A9%202024.pdf): échelon durations 1, 1, 2, 2, 2.5, 3, 3, 3.5, 4, 4 years; 11th échelon reached after 26 years at the standard pace (24 with both accelerations).

- Cumulative time to reach the 9th échelon at standard pace = 1+1+2+2+2.5+3+3+3.5 = **18 years** (my calculation from the grid); RDV 3 falls in year 19 to 20 of service, or 1 to 2 years earlier with accelerations.
- Age: with entry around 25 to 27 (after a master's degree and the concours), RDV 3 falls at roughly **43 to 47 years old**. This is my estimate, not an official figure, and reclassement on entry (prior service) moves it earlier. The note's claim of "~40-45" is in the right range but slightly young for a standard career.

### A3. Is "after the 9th échelon, no more inspection" accurate?

**Broadly yes for compulsory, career-linked inspection. Not literally.**

| After RDV 3 | What happens | Classroom inspection required? |
|---|---|---|
| Hors-classe promotion | Uses the RDV 3 appréciation, kept until promotion | No |
| Classe exceptionnelle (open from the 5th hors-classe échelon, 4th for agrégés) | Recteur's appraisal on the I-Prof file, informed by **avis of the inspector and of the chef d'établissement** (Excellent to À consolider / "très favorable"…); avis cannot be appealed | No inspection required by the texts I found; avis can be written from the file (Reims 2024 https://www.reims.snes.edu/IMG/pdf/240325_acces_classe_exceptionnelle_prof_agreges.pdf ; Grenoble 2025 https://www.grenoble.snes.edu/IMG/pdf/2025-243_circulaire_classe_ex_et_hc.pdf ; Paris https://www.ac-paris.fr/media/58665/download). Whether inspectors visit before an avis: **UNVERIFIED**, varies by academy |
| "4th RDV" | Mentioned in a 2017 Martinique academy presentation as linked to classe exceptionnelle. Not in the national texts as summarised by other circulars | Treat as **UNVERIFIED / probably inaccurate** |
| Ad hoc inspection | Possible: "accompagnement", teacher's request, difficulties or complaints, newly arrived teachers | Not scheduled |

Since décret n° 2024-727 du 6 juillet 2024, recteurs (not the minister) draw up the classe exceptionnelle tables for agrégés (Reims 2025 https://www.reims.snes.edu/IMG/pdf/250303_acces_classe_exceptionnelle_prof_agreges.pdf).

**Suggested wording for the note:** "Depuis 2017, un enseignant n'a plus d'inspection obligatoire après son troisième rendez-vous de carrière, à la deuxième année du 9e échelon, soit environ 19 ans de carrière. Il peut lui rester plus de 20 ans à enseigner sans aucune visite programmée."

### A4. Frequency and cost of inspection

- Cour des comptes (as quoted by AEF, 9 March 2018, via SGEN-CFDT https://sgen-cfdt.fr/contenu/uploads/2018/03/9-03-18.pdf): **170,000 inspections** (year not stated); one inspection = 50 to 60% of a working day, so 85,000 to 102,000 days, or **400 to 490 FTE, 15 to 20% of available IEN and IA-IPR time**. Cour: "Il est anormal que non seulement les inspections aient peu de conséquences sur la gestion du corps enseignant, mais également sur les aspects pédagogiques". The source report is probably the Cour's report on teacher evaluation or "Gérer les enseignants autrement" (Oct. 2017): **UNVERIFIED which report**.
- The same AEF note says the Cour stresses that, unlike many countries, French teacher evaluation does not use pupils' progress and rarely uses parents' or pupils' views.
- Historical frequency before PPCR: commonly cited as once every 6 to 7 years in secondary, with wide variation (some teachers not inspected for over 10 years). **UNVERIFIED**: I could not reach a primary source this session. The SNES Lille 2017 slides note the "fréquence aléatoire des inspections" before the reform (https://lille.snes.edu/IMG/pdf/diaporama_comparatif_nouvelle_evaluation_outil_lutte_pedagogique_jfc_mars2017.pdf).

| Teachers per inspector, secondary public, 2020 | 137 (Limoges) to 228 (Versailles) | same |
| Share of inspector activity on individual teacher evaluation | ~30% (primary), 20 to 30% (secondary) | same |
| IA-IPR recruitment 2023 | 78 of 80 posts filled | concours report 2023 (via search summary; ministry page blocked) |

Split IEN vs IA-IPR: **not found** this session. Order of magnitude often cited: ~1,600 IA-IPR and ~2,100 IEN (**UNVERIFIED**).

At ~229 teachers per primary inspector, one inspection per teacher per year would mean ~229 inspections per inspector a year, roughly one per working day. Every two years: ~115 a year. At 50 to 60% of a day each (Cour figure), that is 57 to 69 days, about **30 to 35% of a ~200-day year**. Today individual evaluation already takes ~30% of activity, so a 1-to-2-year cycle needs either more inspectors, shorter visits, or delegation (peers, chefs d'établissement). This is my arithmetic: state it as an order of magnitude only.

### A6. Missions: the "morcellement"

- **Cour des comptes, référé "Les inspecteurs territoriaux des 1er et 2nd degrés de l'Éducation nationale"** (sent Jan. 2022, published 3 May 2022): "L'activité des inspecteurs est de plus en plus morcelée et cet éparpillement s'effectue au détriment de leur mission première, qui reste le suivi des enseignants et l'appui pédagogique" (quoted by SNCI-FO https://fo-fnecfp.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/22-05-05-Refere-de-la-cour-des-comptes.pdf). Teacher monitoring has become the "variable d'ajustement" of their activity.
  - Missions listed: teacher evaluation, organising exams and concours, promoting national reforms, checking home schooling (instruction en famille), administrative and pedagogical steering of primary schools with directors and municipalities (Acteurs publics, above).
  - Individual evaluation leaves little room for team meetings, advisory visits and support for new teachers.
  - 6 recommendations, including: **"revue sélective des missions"** to refocus on pedagogical support for teachers and teams; a target headcount per academy; functional rapprochement of primary and secondary inspection corps.
  - Ministry answer (Dec 2022): a review of inspector missions "à partir de 2023" (Sénat QE above). Outcome **not found**.
- **IGEN-IGAENR, "Rôle et positionnement des inspecteurs du second degré en académie"** (Oct. 2016, after the circular of 11 Dec 2015 on inspectors' missions): the job of IA-IPR / IEN ET-EG / IEN IO has gained academic steering and support to établissements on top of inspection, and needs to be "mieux équilibré et régulé" (https://www.education.gouv.fr/igesr/role-et-positionnement-des-inspecteurs-du-second-degre-en-academie-463599, page blocked; content from search summary and Acteurs publics "les inspecteurs ont la pression" https://acteurspublics.fr/articles/a-leducation-nationale-les-inspecteurs-ont-la-pression/).
- **Référents laïcité, EAC, numérique**: I found no official document listing these as inspector missions. In practice many academies name IA-IPR or IEN as référents (laïcité et fait religieux, EAC, numérique, égalité filles-garçons, harcèlement…). Keep this claim illustrative and **UNVERIFIED** unless an academy organigram is cited.

## B. School (établissement) evaluation in France: the CEE

- **Legal basis**: loi n° 2019-791 du 26 juillet 2019 "pour une école de la confiance" created the Conseil d'évaluation de l'école (CEE), which sets the method and tools for self-evaluation and external evaluation of schools and ensures they happen "selon une périodicité régulière" (CEE framework, via https://www.education.gouv.fr/le-conseil-d-evaluation-de-l-ecole/conseil-d-evaluation-de-l-ecole-l-evaluation-des-ecoles-et-des-etablissements-du-second-degre-340814). The CEE replaced the Cnesco as the official evaluator. Its members include ministry representatives, MPs and qualified persons (Banque des territoires, 14 Mar 2022).
- **Cycle**: recteurs schedule evaluations so that all public and private-under-contract établissements are evaluated **every 5 years, ~20% a year** (IH2EF https://www.ih2ef.gouv.fr/evaluation-des-etablissements-scolaires). Second-degree framework adopted 8 July 2020, updated 8 June 2023, methodological guide for external evaluation Jan. 2024. Primary-school framework adopted 5 Feb 2026.
- **Process**: (1) self-evaluation by the school community (staff, pupils, parents, partners); (2) external evaluation: "analyse distanciée des indicateurs et des pièces du dossier d'auto-évaluation, une observation des lieux et des pratiques et des échanges" with stakeholders (CEE cahier des charges); (3) report and contract/project.
- **Who evaluates externally**: "cadres mobilisés par les évaluations en académie", trained by IH2EF with the CEE (IH2EF). In practice small teams of inspectors (IA-IPR, IEN) and personnels de direction from other schools, with a deontology charter (https://www.education.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/document/charte-d-ontologie-eval-externe-2d-pdf-518858.pdf). The exact team composition is **UNVERIFIED**; I could not open the Jan. 2024 guide. Key point for the note: **external evaluators are the same inspectors who inspect teachers, plus peer heads**. There is no separate, dedicated corps.
- **Progress**:

| Campaign | Coverage | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2020-21 (1st) | ~950 collèges and lycées, just over 9% | Banque des territoires, 14 Mar 2022 https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/evaluation-des-colleges-et-lycees-les-collectivites-encore-trop-discretes |
| 2021-22 | ~735 primary schools evaluated (CEE délibération 2023-01) | via search summary of education.gouv.fr |
| to 2023-24 | ~80% of secondary établissements; ~34% of primary schools | CEE bilan 2023-24 https://www.education.gouv.fr/le-conseil-d-evaluation-de-l-ecole/bilan-national-de-la-campagne-d-evaluation-des-ecoles-et-des-etablissements-2023-2024-470429 |
| to 2024-25 | ~90% of secondary établissements since 2020; 54% of primary schools since 2021-22 (19% in 2024-25 alone) | CEE bilan 2024-25 https://www.education.gouv.fr/le-conseil-d-evaluation-de-l-ecole/bilan-national-de-la-campagne-d-evaluation-des-ecoles-et-des-etablissements-2024-2025-470426 |
| 2025-26 | 2nd five-year cycle begins | IH2EF colloquium, 19-21 May 2025 https://www.ih2ef.gouv.fr/evaluation-des-etablissements-bilan-et-perspectives-20252030-le-colloque-du-cee |

- **Limits**: reports are not graded and not systematically published school by school (**UNVERIFIED**; check the CEE). Local authorities were poorly informed and rarely involved (Banque des territoires 2022). Inspector unions call the evaluation "chronophage", saying it pulls IEN away from their circonscriptions (SNCI-FO, May 2022). Evaluation of outcomes for the CEE system: **none found**.

## C. England: Ofsted and in-school appraisal

### C1. Framework timeline

| Date | Change | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sept 2014 | Ofsted stops grading individual lessons; inspectors record evidence only | Tes Aug 2014 https://www.tes.com/magazine/archive/ofsted-scraps-grades-individual-lessons ; Ofsted note https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/20770/1/Information%20for%20teachers%20about%20inspection%20-%20lesson%20observations.pdf |
| Sept 2019 | Education Inspection Framework (EIF): 4 judgements (quality of education, behaviour and attitudes, personal development, leadership) + overall grade 1-4 (Outstanding, Good, Requires improvement, Inadequate) | **UNVERIFIED** this session (well known) |
| Jan 2023 | Death of head teacher Ruth Perry after her school was downgraded from Outstanding to Inadequate (safeguarding); inquest Dec 2023 found the inspection contributed to her death | Express & Star / ITV, 2024 https://www.itv.com/news/meridian/2023-12-11/we-must-build-on-ruth-perrys-legacy-to-prevent-repeat-tragedy-keegan |
| 2 Sept 2024 | Government scraps single-word overall judgements, with immediate effect; Gilbert learning review published 3 Sept 2024 | https://www.expressandstar.com/uk-news/one-word-ofsted-judgments-for-schools-scrapped-by-government-5769180 |
| 10 Nov 2025 | Renewed EIF with **report cards**; routine state-school inspections resumed 1 Dec 2025; toolkit v2.0 applies from 7 Sept 2026 | https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/education-inspection-framework/education-inspection-framework-for-use-from-november-2025 |

### C2. The 2025 report card (primary source: gov.uk EIF Nov 2025)

- **Evaluation areas**: Inclusion; Curriculum and teaching; Achievement; Attendance and behaviour; Personal development and well-being; Leadership and governance; Early years and Post-16 where relevant; **Safeguarding = met / not met**.
- **5-point scale per area**: Urgent improvement / Needs attention / Expected standard / Strong standard / Exceptional. **No overall grade.** "Needs attention" is presented as an early warning, not a failure (gov.uk "Understanding Ofsted report cards", 1 Dec 2025 https://www.gov.uk/guidance/understanding-ofsted-report-cards-and-grades).
- **Frequency**: routine inspection usually about **every 4 years**. The statutory requirement is within **5 school years** of the end of the year of the last inspection (The Key, 2025-26 https://schoolleaders.thekeysupport.com/school-evaluation-and-improvement/inspection/after-inspection/frequency-inspection-overview/ ; Waltham Forest letter to parents Jan 2026). These are secondary sources, consistent with each other.
- **Monitoring** (Ofsted monitoring operating guide, Nov 2025 https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/school-inspection-toolkit-operating-guide-and-information/school-monitoring-operating-guide-for-inspectors-for-use-from-november-2025): "needs attention" schools get a monitoring inspection **no later than 24 months** after the report card; schools in a category of concern can get **up to 5 on-site monitoring inspections within 18 months**. This is risk-based targeting.
- **Teams**: 2 inspectors per day in small schools, 3 on day 1 otherwise (Ofsted blog FAQ, 11 Dec 2025 https://educationinspection.blog.gov.uk/2025/12/11/school-inspections-frequently-asked-questions/).
- **Inspectors**: employed **His Majesty's Inspectors** (~343 HMI + 88 Senior HMI, FDA union survey May 2025 https://www.fda.org.uk/news/new-research-reveals-98-per-cent-of-his-majestys-inspectors-are-overworked, not an official count) plus **contracted Ofsted Inspectors**, many of them **serving practitioners** (heads and senior leaders). Contracting was brought in-house in 2015 to raise the share of teams with a serving leader (Ofsted Annual Report 2014-15). Current share of serving practitioners: **not found**.
- **No grading of individual teachers**: Ofsted grades the school, not teachers or lessons, since 2014.

### C3. Teacher appraisal (done by the school, not Ofsted)

- **Education (School Teachers' Appraisal) (England) Regulations 2012** (SI 2012/115, in force Sept 2012): annual appraisal of every teacher by the head teacher or a delegate, with written objectives, classroom observation and a written report including a pay recommendation (legislation.gov.uk https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/115, **UNVERIFIED** this session; SI 2012/2055 only amended the induction exclusion https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/2055).
- **2024**: the School Teachers' Pay and Conditions Document 2024 removed the requirement that **all pay progression be linked to appraisal**, from 1 Sept 2024. Appraisal remains compulsory (school policy quoting STPCD 2024 https://www.kingedwin.notts.sch.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Appraisal-Policy-for-Teachers.pdf). Check the STPCD text before quoting.
- **Takeaway**: England **separates** school inspection (external, Ofsted, every ~4 years) from teacher appraisal (internal, annual, by line managers).

### D2. Causal evidence that evaluation itself improves teaching

| Study | Setting | Design | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taylor & Tyler (2012), *AER* 102(7): 3628-51 https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.102.7.3628 | Cincinnati TES, mid-career teachers | Within-teacher, before/after a scheduled evaluation year; observed 4 times in the year (3 by **external peer evaluators**, high-performing experienced teachers; 1 by the principal); evaluated year 1, year 4, then **every 5 years** | Students of a teacher after TES score **~10% of a SD higher in math** than the same teacher's students before; gains persist and are largest for initially weak teachers (NBER WP 16877 text) |
| Steinberg & Sartain (2015), *Education Finance and Policy* 10(4): 535-72 https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/edfpol/v10y2015i4p535-572.html | Chicago Excellence in Teaching Project, principal-teacher observation and dialogue (Danielson framework) | Randomised staggered rollout, 44 vs 48 elementary schools (2008-09 / 2009-10) | Cohort 1 outperformed in reading after year 1 (math not significant); gains concentrated in higher-achieving, lower-poverty schools. Magnitude ~0.10 SD reading: **UNVERIFIED** |
| Burgess, Rawal & Taylor (2021), *J. Labor Econ.* 39(4): 1155-86 https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jlabec/doi10.1086-712997.html | 82 English secondary schools, teachers observe and score each other (EEF-funded) | School-level RCT; teachers randomly assigned to observer/observee | **+0.07 SD** in GCSE maths and English; both roles benefit, observers possibly more; **doubling observations did not raise effects**; larger for low-performing teachers |

Reading for the note: the gains come from **structured observation with a rubric and feedback**, even when stakes are low (Burgess et al.) and even when done by peers (Cincinnati, England). Frequency matters less than quality: doubling observations added nothing in England, and Cincinnati's effects come from one intensive year every 5 years.

## Summary: the most useful comparisons for a French note

1. Since PPCR (2017), French teachers have 3 compulsory inspections, at the 6th, 8th and 9th échelons; the last falls around year 19 of a career (~43-47 years old, my estimate), leaving 20+ years without a scheduled visit. The claim is accurate in substance; say "après le 3e rendez-vous de carrière (9e échelon)".
2. After that, hors-classe uses the kept RDV 3 appraisal and classe exceptionnelle uses file-based avis from the inspector and the head: no compulsory classroom visit.
3. Capacity is the binding constraint: ~3,800 inspectors (2021), 229 primary teachers per inspector (2020), and individual evaluation already takes 20-30% of their time. A 1-to-2-year cycle needs about 30-35% of inspector time (my arithmetic) or delegation.
4. The Cour des comptes (May 2022) already diagnosed "morcellement" and asked for a "revue sélective des missions" to refocus on pedagogical support. Use it as the authority for refocusing.
5. TALIS 2024: French teachers get less useful feedback from principals than the OECD average (71% vs 77%), and fewer novices have a mentor (17% vs 26%).
6. School evaluation exists (CEE, 2019 law, every 5 years, ~90% of secondary schools covered by 2024-25), but it is run by the same inspectors and heads. A separate specialised body would be new; the closest models are Ofsted and the Dutch Inspectie.
7. England separates the two jobs: Ofsted inspects schools (~every 4 years, 5-point report card by area since Nov 2025, no teacher grading since 2014); heads appraise every teacher annually (2012 regulations).
8. Ofsted's risk-based targeting (monitoring within 24 months for "needs attention", up to 5 visits in 18 months for schools of concern) suits a French school-evaluation body better than a uniform cycle.
9. Causal evidence: being failed by Ofsted raises scores ~0.1 SD (Allen & Burgess 2012; Hussain 2015). Structured teacher evaluation raises student scores 0.07-0.10 SD (Taylor & Tyler 2012; Burgess et al. 2021).
10. Design lesson: what works is rubric-based observation with feedback, often by trained peers; extra frequency adds little (doubling observations: no gain). A "every 1-2 years" pledge is more credible as frequent formative visits (peers, heads, conseillers pédagogiques) plus a rarer inspector evaluation, than as a full inspection every year.
