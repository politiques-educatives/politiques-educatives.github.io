### Verdict
- **Most likely candidate: the ISTAT survey released December 2018 / January 2019 (a.s. 2017-18).**
  It is the only "2018" document, and its headline (one support teacher in three untrained) is
  the one press coverage repeats. **Confidence: moderate, about 50 %.** If the user remembers the
  continuity figures (more than half of pupils changing teacher every year), the 2018-19 edition
  (published Feb 2020) is the better match, and it is the one read in full here. The 2011
  Agnelli-Treellle-Caritas report is the main *critical* study and is often misdated.
- **What all of them show, beyond the existing I2 row:** Italy's problem is not staff numbers
  (1.6 pupils per support teacher, better than the legal norm) but **training and stability**:
  a third untrained, more than half changing teacher each year, and support often delivered
  outside the class (the "delega" problem). No source shows learning outcomes.

### ASD Nest and Horizon (autism programmes in ordinary district schools)
- **Nest** (launched 2003, NYU + DOE): an ICT class inside a mainstream school, two teachers (one
  general, one special education), **no paraprofessional**. Kindergarten **12 pupils, 4 autistic**;
  grades 1-3 **16, 4 autistic**; grades 4-5 **20, up to 5 autistic** (Autism Spectrum News;
  InsideSchools gives 4:16 for grades 4-5). Plus NYU's **Social Development Intervention (SDI)**
  taught in small groups by related-service staff, and specialised teacher training.
  [NYU Nest](https://steinhardt.nyu.edu/metrocenter/nest/new-york-city);
  [Autism Spectrum News](https://autismspectrumnews.org/the-asd-nest-a-successful-inclusion-model-in-the-nyc-public-school-system/)
- **Horizon**: special class, **8:1:1** (8 autistic pupils, 1 special-education teacher, 1 para),
  ABA-informed, inside district schools. [MOPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/mopd/resources/school-programs-resources.page)
- **Scale:** Nest in 59 schools with >1 700 pupils (2021-22, secondary) and "more than 90 schools"
  (NYU, current). All specialised autism programmes: **~5 100 pupils** projected, **+16 classes / 116
  seats** for 2025-26 (DOE, Council finance hearing 20 May 2025). A **97 % four-year graduation
  rate** for Nest and Horizon was asserted by a council member: UNVERIFIED.
  [citymeetings.nyc](https://citymeetings.nyc/meetings/new-york-city-council/2025-05-20-1000-am-committee-on-finance/chapter/discussion-on-nest-and-horizon-programs-for-children-with-autism/)
- **Evaluation: none causal.** The Research Alliance for NYC Schools did a qualitative study (29
  staff, 3 schools chosen for fidelity) and is running *Taking Flight*, a 3-year survey of Nest
  graduates (2025-2027) benchmarked on the US NLTS-2; no results yet.
  [qualitative](https://steinhardt.nyu.edu/research-alliance/research/asd-nest-exploring-educators-perspectives-innovative-model-autistic);
  [Taking Flight](https://steinhardt.nyu.edu/research-alliance/taking-flight-exploring-nest-student-outcomes-after-high-school)
- **Selection caveat:** Nest admits autistic pupils judged able to follow the general curriculum
  (cognitive and behavioural criteria), so raw outcomes are not comparable with other settings.

### 2012 reform: *A Shared Path to Success*
- Pilot 2010, citywide **September 2012**. Principle: zoned schools must serve pupils with IEPs
  (more access to general curriculum), with funding following the pupil and flexible IEPs.
  Prompted partly by a 2012 Public Advocate report on low inclusion and graduation (secondary).
  [Hubert 2013](https://afterbloomberg.commons.gc.cuny.edu/files/2013/05/Hubert.pdf);
  [Chalkbeat 2013](https://chalkbeat.org/posts/ny/2013/10/28/new-data-provide-a-glimpse-at-citywide-special-ed-reform-efforts)
- **Implementation:** teachers' union complaints of missing aides and ICT classes where most pupils
  had disabilities, teachers "overwhelmed" ([EdWeek, Oct 2014](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/teacher-complaints-follow-n-y-c-special-education-overhaul/2014/10)).
- **Evaluation:** Stiefel, Gottfried, Shiferaw and Schwartz, *Is Special Education Improving? Case
  Evidence from New York City* (2005-2015 data): inclusion rose in elementary and middle school,
  **fell in high school**; graduation of pupils with disabilities rose but stayed at **~50 % vs
  ~80 %** for others (figures secondary); descriptive, not causal.
  [NYU Wagner](https://wagner.nyu.edu/impact/research/publications/special-education-improving-case-evidence-new-york-city).
  The Research Alliance did not evaluate the reform itself; its 2019 brief finds autism, emotional
  disturbance and intellectual disability mostly placed in self-contained classes.
  [brief](https://steinhardt.nyu.edu/research-alliance/research/publications/special-education-new-york-city)
- **State finding, 2019:** the NY State Education Department's Compliance Assurance Plan said the
  city had violated federal law (IDEA) for 13 years and past reforms had "not resulted in the
  systemic change necessary" (secondary). [Chalkbeat, 9 Jul 2019](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2019/7/9/21108488/nyc-vows-to-address-special-education-failures-detailed-in-state-review-but-will-their-reforms-go-fa)

## C. Québec

### Framework
- **Politique de l'adaptation scolaire (1999)**: ordinary class, near home, is the first option when
  it helps learning and social integration; otherwise special class or school. The **plan
  d'intervention** is mandatory, drawn up by the principal with parents, staff and pupil (Loi sur
  l'instruction publique, art. 96.14). A pupil is counted as **EHDAA/HDAA** when a plan
  d'intervention is declared at 30 September: the count is administrative, not a prevalence
  (secondary). [CISSS Lanaudière](https://www.cisss-lanaudiere.gouv.qc.ca/fileadmin/internet/cisss_lanaudiere/Documentation/Sylia_statistiques_regionales/Scolarite_diplomation_et_decrochage/EHDAA.pdf)
- **Two categories, two funding logics.** *Élèves handicapés* (codes 14 to 99: autism, language
  disorder, intellectual disability, etc.) need a professional evaluation matching the diagnosis
  to obtain a code, which triggers extra funding. *Élèves en difficulté (EDAA)* need no
  diagnosis and are funded through general allocations (secondary,
  [FCPQ 2021](https://www.fcpq.qc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/FCPQ_Avis_Projet294_20210324.pdf)).
  The CDPDJ and Protecteur du citoyen both reject this diagnosis-based funding and ask for a
  needs-based model and a funded **minimum threshold of services**; the ministry was piloting a
  revision in 2023 (CDPDJ 2023 synthesis, primary).
- **Classes langage** (for *déficience langagière*) and TSA classes exist as special classes at
  school-board level; no provincial ratio was found (UNVERIFIED).

### Debates and failures
- **CDPDJ (2018) systemic study** (Ducharme and Magloire), 22 recommendations: right to education
  "still not acquired"; 73 % of CDPDJ education investigations since 1999 concern HDAA pupils (642
  files). In 2023 most recommendations were still not implemented. [synthesis, 2023](https://www.cdpdj.qc.ca/storage/app/media/publications/synthese_colloque-EHDAA.pdf);
  [study](https://cdpdj.qc.ca/storage/app/media/vos-droits/qu-est-ce-que/droits-des-eleves-HDAA/etude_inclusion_EHDAA.pdf) (not read)
- **Protecteur du citoyen (June 2022)**, *L'élève avant tout*, on 86 000 EDAA primary pupils:
  services organised by available resources, not need; **up to 8 months' wait for a first
  evaluation, then up to 8 months for the service**, often more than a school year in total;
  **39 % of parents told by the school to get a private evaluation, 42 % did**; unstable
  assignments of support staff; ministry has no data on vacant professional posts (as reported in
  CDPDJ 2023 synthesis). [report](http://protecteurducitoyen.qc.ca/sites/default/files/2022-06/rapport-special-services-educatifs-adaptes.pdf)
- **Conseil supérieur de l'éducation (Oct 2017)**, *Pour une école riche de tous ses élèves*:
  from a segregated to an increasingly inclusive system in 50 years, but **difficulties are
  increasingly concentrated in ordinary classes** because selective "projets particuliers" and
  special classes regroup pupils; a "point de bascule" reached (the "école à trois vitesses"
  argument). [summary PDF](https://www.cse.gouv.qc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/50-0500-SO-ecole-riche-eleves.pdf)
- **2023-2028 collective agreement** (announced 18 Feb 2024): **4 000 classroom aides** for >14 000
  classes; a new "class composition" mechanism (extra resources "if possible"); TES pay $65 000 →
  $77 000. [Québec.ca](https://www.quebec.ca/nouvelles/actualites/details/negociations-pour-le-renouvellement-des-conventions-collectives-des-secteurs-public-et-parapublic-des-avancees-importantes-pour-leducation-au-quebec-53822).
  The FAE's 2023 demands were "more homogeneous" class composition and more
  *enseignants-orthopédagogues* for early screening. [FAE](https://alliancedesprofs.qc.ca/storage/app/media/votre-vie-syndicale/negos-2023/demandes-syndicales/2023-09-07_fae-priorites-de-negociation.pdf)
- **2025 cuts:** **570 M$** imposed on the network in summer 2025 (partly relaxed); parents'
  federation reports missing services for pupils with special needs at the rentrée 2025 and
  parents paying privately for psychology and special education
  ([Noovo](https://www.noovo.info/nouvelle/budgets-en-education-lebel-veut-accompagner-les-centres-de-services-scolaires.html);
  [FCPQ](https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/min/finances/publications-adm/Consultations_prebudgetaires/2026-2027/Federation_comites_parents.pdf)).
  A national count of new "classes spécialisées" in 2024-25: not found (UNVERIFIED).

## D. Lessons for France

| # | Lesson | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stability of the adult matters as much as numbers.** Italy has 1.6 pupils per support teacher, yet 57.6 % change teacher each year and 10 % mid-year. France's AESH turnover is the same risk. Tie support staff to a school (not a pupil) for at least 3 years. | ISTAT 2018-19; Protecteur 2022 (unstable assignments) |
| 2 | **Train before you hire.** One Italian support teacher in three is untrained (47 % in the North) despite a specialised diploma track. A support role without a required qualification fills with the untrained when demand grows. | ISTAT 2017-18, 2018-19 |
| 3 | **Put specialised units inside ordinary schools, with two teachers.** NYC's Nest (12-20 pupils, 4-5 autistic, two teachers, social-skills curriculum) and Horizon (8:1:1) are the closest analogues to UEMA/ULIS-TSA; demand keeps rising (+116 seats in 2025-26). But no causal evaluation exists: France should evaluate its own units from the start. | NYU; DOE 2025; Research Alliance |
| 4 | **Co-teaching needs a cap on concentration.** NYC caps pupils with disabilities at 40 % of an ICT class; where caps slipped after 2012, teachers were overwhelmed. Consistent with the 15-20 % threshold in the Swiss study already in the review [E2]. | Chalkbeat 2020; EdWeek 2014 |
| 5 | **Supply creates placement.** In NYC, a pupil is more likely to get ICT if the school has an ICT class; placements rarely change. French notifications should be reviewed against what is available locally, and units placed where the waiting lists are. | IBO 2024 |
| 6 | **Diagnosis-gated funding produces waiting lists and a private market.** Québec: up to 16 months from need to service, 42 % of parents pay privately; both watchdogs ask for needs-based funding. France's MDPH gate (21 months for an IME place) is the same mechanism. Fund a first level of help (orthophonie, psychologist) without waiting for the notification. | Protecteur 2022; CDPDJ 2018/2023 |
| 7 | **Enforceable rights without supply shift money to lawyers.** NYC: 21 000 due-process complaints a year and $1.3 bn in private tuition, with racial gaps in who wins. France's rising referrés over AESH hours risk the same drift; invest in supply first. | Manhattan Institute 2026; Chalkbeat 2026; ISTAT (6 % TAR appeals) |

Caveat for all lessons: none of the three systems has a causal evaluation of its inclusion model.
Québec's HDAA diploma gap (49.4 % vs ~88 %) and NYC's (~50 % vs ~80 %) describe outcomes, not the
effect of settings.

## Summary
- **Italy:** most likely "2018 study" = ISTAT inclusion survey a.s. 2017-18 (released Dec 2018 / Jan
  2019), ~50 % confidence; the 2018-19 edition (Feb 2020) has the sharpest numbers: 1.6 pupils per
  support teacher, 36 % untrained, 57.6 % change teacher yearly, 6 % of families go to court.
  The 2011 Agnelli-Treellle-Caritas report is the main critique, often misdated.
- **NYC:** 23.2 % of pupils have an IEP; ~half of them in two-teacher ICT classes; Nest/Horizon
  autism units (~5 100 pupils) popular but unevaluated; 2012 reform raised inclusion except in
  high school; litigation exploded (21 000 complaints, $1.3 bn Carter costs).
- **Québec:** 23.9 % HDAA, 78.2 % in ordinary classes; HDAA diploma rate 49.4 % after 7 years;
  watchdogs blame diagnosis-based funding and waits of up to 16 months.
