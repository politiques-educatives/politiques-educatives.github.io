## A. France

### A1. Where 3e pupils go (campaign 2025, DGESCO *Repères pour l'orientation et l'affectation des élèves 2025*, primary source)
Source: https://eduscol.education.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/document/reperes-pour-l-orientation-et-l-affectation-des-eleves-campagne-2025-126290.pdf (666,637 pupils in public 3e; 28.4% hold a bourse)

| 2025, all public 3e | 2de GT | 2de pro | 1re année CAP | Total voie pro |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Family requests (final phase) | 63.8% | 25.5% | 10.7% | 36.2% |
| Final decisions | 61.4% | 27.6% | 11.0% | **38.6%** |
| Boursiers: final decisions | 47.8% | — | — | **52.2%** |
| Non-boursiers: final decisions | 66.7% | — | — | 33.3% |

- **Class councils push pupils towards voie pro.** Decisions to 2de GT run 2.4 points below requests. For boursiers, the gap between first intentions and final decisions is 9.3 points, against 5.5 for non-boursiers. Boursiers "adjust" downward between the two phases: their choice of 2de GT falls 5.4 points, against 3.6 for non-boursiers (Repères 2025, pp. 6–9).
- **Provisional advice is the main channel.** For boursiers, class councils give 58.8% provisional advice to voie pro, against 37.7% for non-boursiers (p. 9).
- **Gender.** Final decisions to 2de GT: girls 68.3%, boys 54.8% (graph 25).
- **Apprenticeship.** 6.8% of 3e pupils made a final choice of apprenticeship in 2025, up 2 points on 2024. The figure is 4.2% for girls (pp. 27–29).
- **First wishes in voie pro are hard to get.** In 2025 only **58.1%** of rank-1 wishes in voie pro were satisfied (2de pro 61.3%, CAP 49.9%), against **87.0%** in 2de GT. Lycée professionnel places fell by 3,391 in 2025, and the document states that "la capacité d'accueil en lycée professionnel demeure insuffisante pour répondre à la demande" (table 2, p. ~12).
- **Formal disagreement is rare.** 2.5% of 3e decisions matched none of the family's requests (15,954 out of 648,880). Families can appeal within 3 working days, or claim a one-year *maintien* (keeping the pupil in the same class) under art. D.331-35. In 17.2% of 3e disagreements the family asked for maintien (graphs 34–35, p. 63). **Implication for the recteur's point:** redoublement exists only as a family right after a disagreement, not as an option the school team offers.
- **Trend.** DEPP NI 23.40 (Sept. 2023): orientation to 2de GT rose from 61% (3e cohort 2010-11) to 69% (2019-20). The choice between GT and pro "reste fortement lié au passé scolaire ainsi qu'à l'origine sociale", and pupils going to voie pro "ont très majoritairement les résultats scolaires les plus faibles au collège". The two figures are not comparable: NI 23.40 counts cohorts and includes the private sector, while Repères counts the public sector only. https://www.education.gouv.fr/depp/l-orientation-en-fin-de-troisieme-reste-marquee-par-de-fortes-disparites-scolaires-et-sociales-452367
- Géographie de l'École 2021 (DEPP), 3e pupils of 2019 one year later: 64% GT, 28% pro as pupils, 5% apprenticeship, 3% repeat (via the DEPP search snippet; the PDF is not checked, so **UNVERIFIED**).

### A2. Affelnet and the weight of grades
- Affelnet-Lycée ranks candidates for each wish with a points score. The score has two separate parts: mastery of the *socle commun* (8 components on 4 levels) and 3e grades, converted to a common scale. Bonuses for address, bourse status and, in Paris, the collège's IPS (indice de position sociale) are added depending on the académie. Sources: ac-Réunion technical note 2025, https://www.ac-reunion.fr/sites/ac_reunion/files/2025-04/note-technique-gestion-des-valuations-affelnet-lyc-e-2025-pour-les-coll-ges-43822.pdf ; Grenoble memento 2017 (socle levels 10/25/40/50 points × coefficient 30, old), https://sacoche.sesamath.net/_doc/DNB_Affelnet_2017_memento_Grenoble_extrait.pdf
- **For voie pro, grades decide.** Selective 2de pro specialities are filled on the grade score. Because demand exceeds places (58.1% first-wish satisfaction), a fall in grades in 3e directly costs a pupil the speciality they wanted. The recteur's point is consistent with this mechanism. No national study isolates the effect of the 3e grade drop, so that specific claim stays **UNVERIFIED**.

### A3. Social composition of the voie pro
- Rentrée 2023, public sector: children of manual workers (*ouvriers*) or inactive parents make up **55.2%** of pupils in lycée professional courses (53.3% in bac pro, 64.8% in CAP), against **29.3%** in GT. INSEE TEF from MEN-DEPP data: https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/8242339
- Cour des comptes, RPA 2025 (Rapport public annuel 2025): "à notes et vœux équivalents, les décisions des équipes éducatives sont influencées par le contexte social de l'élève"; "le processus d'orientation tend donc plutôt à amplifier le déterminisme social". Secondary report of the chapter: https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/lorientation-une-politique-sans-boussole

### A4. Drop-out and re-orientation in the voie pro
DEPP NI 26.24 (June 2026) follows 169,700 pupils who entered 2de pro in 2019 after sitting the DNB (diplôme national du brevet): https://www.education.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/document/deppni-2026-24parcours-scolairesokpdf-517766.pdf
- 89% were in 1re pro in 2020, so about 11% left, re-oriented or repeated after the first year. 83% reached terminale pro by 2021. 64% obtained the bac by 2023.
- By 2021, 16% of those who failed the DNB had left school, against 7% of those who passed without mention. Pupils who failed the DNB re-orient mostly towards CAP. One in five pupils of the whole cohort did not sit the bac in 2022.
- Odds of obtaining the bac rise steeply with the DNB score (odds ratio 2.66 for a score of 18–20 vs 10–11).
- Apprentices are a separate measure: 42% of CAP-level contracts are broken within the first 18 months (DARES, 2018 entrants), which counts contract breaks, not drop-outs. https://nouvelle-aquitaine.dreets.gouv.fr/sites/nouvelle-aquitaine.dreets.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/art_actualites_etude_dares_160125.pdf (via search snippet)
- No national DEPP figure for drop-out in the 1st year of school-based CAP was found. **UNVERIFIED** for CAP.

### A5. Geography and rural youth
- **INSEE Première n°1888 (Jan. 2022), census 2018.** 93.8% of rural 15–17-year-olds attend school outside their commune, and their average distance to the lycée is **23.2 km**, double the collège distance (11.3 km). In very sparse rural communes the distance is 1.7 times that in rural communes close to a town. Rural 16–24-year-olds are more often apprentices. https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/6035523 (PDF IP1888). This **confirms the recteur's "~20 km"**.
- **DEPP NI 23.19 (2023), *indice d'éloignement* (remoteness index).** The most remote lycées are more often agricultural or small public lycées, more often have a boarding house (*internat*), and "la voie professionnelle y est plus souvent représentée". https://www.education.gouv.fr/depp/une-mesure-de-l-eloignement-des-lycees-452292 (page 403; content via search snippet)
- **Berlioux report (5 March 2020)**, *Orientation et égalité des chances dans la France des zones rurales et des petites villes*. It concerns about 10 million young people and points to deficits in information, transport and networks. In the IFOP survey for the report, 48% of 17–23-year-olds in small towns had done or planned "ambitious" higher studies, against 67% in the Paris agglomeration. https://www.centre-inffo.fr/?p=349614 (the report PDF returned 404, so figures come from secondary coverage)
- Eduscol: rural families "privilégient davantage une orientation de proximité géographique vers la voie professionnelle" (the *Territoires éducatifs ruraux* programme). https://eduscol.education.gouv.fr/4818/territoires-ruraux-et-de-montagne
- Cour des comptes RPA 2025: 85% of rural youth are satisfied with their lycée assignment, "comme ailleurs" (Banque des Territoires, above). **Nuance:** the problem lies in the choice of courses, not in satisfaction with the assignment.
- Défenseure des droits (3 June 2025): régions "plus soucieuses de pousser les élèves vers les emplois disponibles localement que de favoriser leur mobilité géographique" (L'Humanité, 4 June 2025, press summary of the report *Le droit à l'orientation dans l'enseignement secondaire : un droit à déployer pour tous les jeunes*). https://eno.ombudsman.europa.eu/home/news/archives-2025/maincontent/orientation-scolaire-un-droit-so.html

**Second professeur principal (PP) in terminale (2018).**
- Cnesco survey, Dec. 2018: 89% of lycées had appointed a 2nd PP in terminale, but only one third had held the 2 mandatory orientation weeks (AEF via search snippet, **UNVERIFIED** detail).
- DEPP (Oct. 2021, rentrée 2020): "il est plus compliqué pour un professeur d'enseignement de spécialité d'être professeur principal" (AEF title; the note itself was not read).
- SNES ran a campaign calling on teachers to refuse the PP role (https://www.snes.edu/IMG/pdf/tract_refus_pp.pdf), an advocacy source.
- The Défenseure des droits (2025) notes that PPs were given orientation duties "sans qu'ils bénéficient de la formation nécessaire", and that the hours are "jamais prises en compte dans les emplois du temps".
- IGÉSR annual report 2020 (published Oct. 2021): the PP role is "incontournable" but "limité dans le processus décisionnaire", because pupils rely mainly on family. It proposes orientation modules in initial and in-service training. PsyEN hold permanences in 3 establishments on average. https://www.letudiant.fr/educpros/actualite/l-inspection-generale-dresse-des-pistes-pour-ameliorer-l-orientation.html
- **No source quantifies "few teachers wanted the role".** The claim stays **UNVERIFIED**, although the evidence above is consistent with it.
- IGÉSR synthesis of the national consultation on orientation (report n°24-25 236A, April 2025): https://www.education.gouv.fr/igesr/synthese-de-la-concertation-nationale-sur-l-orientation-des-eleves-465618 (page 403, content not read). The ministry's "plan Avenir" for rentrée 2025 aims to fight "assignations sociales, de genre et territoriales" (Repères 2025, introduction).
- Teachers' knowledge of courses: the Cour (2012 report on orientation at the end of collège) already recommended training teachers on courses and job prospects (recommendation 3). The IGÉSR (2021) and the Défenseure (2025) repeat the point. No survey measures teachers' knowledge directly, so **UNVERIFIED** as a figure.

### A7. Girls and science
- Cour des comptes RPA 2025: **8.6%** girls in terminale STI2D, against 84.5% in ST2S.
- Repères 2025: industrial courses are "essentiellement masculines", tertiary courses "féminines" (introduction, section 3.4).
- **Breda, Grenet, Monnet & Van Effenterre (2023, *Economic Journal* 133:1773–1809).** One-hour classroom visits by 56 female scientists were randomised among 20,000 lycéens in Île-de-France. Among terminale girls, enrolment in selective, male-dominated STEM courses rose from 11% to 14.5%, driven by girls who were strong in maths. There was no effect in 2de. The visits reduced stereotypes, and they worked best when they did not stress women's under-representation. https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-01713068

## B. England

**Who does guidance.** Schools are responsible, under a statutory duty; there is no national counsellor corps in schools.

| Element | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Statutory duty | Governing bodies "must" secure independent, impartial careers guidance for pupils in years 7–13. Legal bases: ss.42A/42B/45A Education Act 1997 and s.72 Education and Skills Act 2008 | DfE statutory guidance (last updated 23 June 2026) https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/careers-guidance-provision-for-young-people-in-schools/careers-guidance-and-access-for-education-and-training-providers |
| History | The Education Act 2011 moved the duty from Connexions to schools (from Sept. 2012, years 9–11), later extended to years 8–13 and then year 7 (2022) | **UNVERIFIED** (background); the current guidance does not restate the history |
| Provider access ("Baker Clause" 2018, strengthened by Skills and Post-16 Education Act 2022, in force Jan. 2023) | **at least 6 encounters** with technical-education or apprenticeship providers: 2 mandatory in Y8–9, 2 mandatory in Y10–11, 2 offered in Y12–13, all in the school day | DfE guidance above; the Baker Clause dates are **UNVERIFIED** |
| Careers Leader | each school "should" appoint a trained careers leader with protected time and a budget | DfE guidance |
| Gatsby Benchmarks | 8 benchmarks (Holman 2014, Gatsby Foundation), updated Nov. 2024 (*Good Career Guidance: The Next 10 Years*), to be used from 2025-26; strengthened benchmark 6 = multiple workplace experiences by 16 plus one by 18; government aims at 2 weeks' work experience guaranteed | DfE guidance; https://gatsbybenchmarks.org.uk/?p=777 |
| Careers hubs (CEC + local authorities) | 96% of schools and colleges in a hub (Sept. 2025) | DfE guidance |
| Achievement | average benchmarks achieved rose from 2 (2016/17) to **6.0/8** (2024/25); more than a quarter achieve all 8; longest-standing hub members 6.2 vs newest 5.6; work experience is the hardest benchmark | CEC insight briefing 2024/25 https://www.careersandenterprise.co.uk/media/dbbboivk/2103-insight-briefing-gatsby-benchmark-results-for-2024-25-v11.pdf |

**Evidence that the benchmarks raise outcomes. It is correlational.**
- CEC (9 June 2021): benchmark achievement in 2017/18 linked to Y11 leavers' destinations in 2018/19. Each extra benchmark is associated with a **1.1%** lower likelihood of a NEET (not in education, employment or training) or unknown destination (re-run over the 2016/17–2018/19 cohorts). The effect is stronger in the most disadvantaged schools. https://www.careersandenterprise.co.uk/evidence-and-reports/the-benefits-of-gatsby-benchmark-achievement-for-post-16-destinations
- CEC *Now and Next* (March 2024): schools achieving all 8 benchmarks are **associated** with an 8% lower NEET likelihood at 16 and at 18. The data are self-reported benchmark achievement from more than 4,500 institutions. https://www.gatsby.org.uk/education/updates/new-national-data-reinforces-the-impact-of-the-gatsby-benchmarks-on-young-peoples-futures/
- Fiscal estimate (Percy, 2025 technical note): ~£300m per year NPV, £1.4bn cumulatively over 2020/21–2024/25. The NEET reductions are "inferred". https://www.careersandenterprise.co.uk/media/3wtgnnaf/technical-note-fiscal-benefit-of-inferred-neet-reductions-for-2020-21-to-2024-25.pdf
- **Hanson, Moore, Neary & Clark (2021, iCeGS Derby)**, North-East pilot 2016–2019 in 16 schools and colleges. Career-readiness scores rose significantly in all year groups. 50% of institutions met no benchmark at the start; two years later 85% met 6 or more. GCSE effects are claimed, but the figures were not checked. **There is no control group, so the design is not causal.** https://repository.derby.ac.uk/item/93xy0/an-evaluation-of-the-north-east-of-england-pilot-of-the-gatsby-benchmarks-of-good-career-guidance
- **Caveat:** all of the above is self-reported or before/after evidence. Schools that achieve all 8 benchmarks may differ in other ways (selection), and none of the studies corrects for this.

**14–16 options and T Levels.** In England the vocational/academic split comes mainly at 16, not at 15. At 14 pupils choose GCSE options; at 16 they choose A-levels, T Levels or an apprenticeship. T Levels (2-year technical, with an industry placement) had 25,508 starts in Sept. 2024 (NAO) and 27,446 in 2025/26, short of DfE's 33,400 estimate. 71% completed the 2-year course (NAO, summer 2024). https://www.nao.org.uk/press-releases/dfe-must-increase-student-numbers-to-realise-full-benefits-of-t-level-qualifications/ ; https://feweek.co.uk/dfe-misses-2025-26-t-level-starts-target-by-nearly-a-fifth/

## C. Finland, Germany, Switzerland

**Finland**
- Guidance (*opinto-ohjaus*) is a right under the Basic Education Act, delivered by specialist guidance counsellors (*opinto-ohjaaja*), not by class teachers. Grades 7–9 have **76 timetabled hours** of guidance (≈2 hours a week), plus individual and small-group counselling. Source: Vuorinen & Kettunen (Jyväskylä), OECD webinar, 28 Oct. 2021, a conference slide rather than the curriculum, so to be confirmed: https://t4.oecd.org/education/career-readiness/conferences-webinars/1.%20Raimo%20Vuorinen,%20Jaana%20Kettunen_OECD_28_10_2021_JK_RV.pdf
- **Intensified guidance in grades 8–9** for pupils who need it, with a personal post-basic study plan (City of Tampere). https://tampere.fi/en/education/basic-education/studies-basic-education/grades-7-through-9/guidance-counselling
- **TET work-experience periods (*työelämään tutustuminen*):** 2 weeks in grade 9, plus shorter 2–5-day periods possible in grades 7–8 (City of Vantaa). https://www.vantaa.fi/en/node/3138
- **Compulsory education extended to 18 (Act in force 1 Aug. 2021).** Pupils must apply to upper secondary, and the basic-education provider is responsible for guidance and for tracking each pupil's transition. In Nov. 2021 only 664 pupils (0.01% of the age group) had no placement. https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/news/finland-extension-compulsory-education-comes-effect
- Guidance in primary school and a pupils-per-counsellor norm: not found, **UNVERIFIED**.

**Germany.** Federal Employment Agency (BA) counsellors work inside schools (*Berufsberatung vor dem Erwerbsleben*, the nationwide *Lebensbegleitende Berufsberatung* since 1 Jan. 2019). Hesse alone has about 270 BA advisers working in schools. Orientation starts about 3 years before leaving school. https://starweb.hessen.de/cache/DRS/20/6/01266.pdf (regional; national figures **UNVERIFIED**)

**Switzerland.** About two-thirds of young people enter VET after the 9 years of compulsory school (OECD). Career choice in lower secondary is structured around trial placements (*Schnupperlehre*, not verified here). https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/vocational-education-and-training-systems-in-nine-countries_1a86eb6c-en/full-report/vocational-education-and-training-in-switzerland_051e4a43.html

## Ten lessons

1. **The 3e decision falls hardest on poor pupils, beyond what grades explain.** 52.2% of boursiers get a voie pro decision against 33.3% of non-boursiers, and boursiers adjust their wishes downward more (9.3 vs 5.5 points) (Repères 2025). The Cour des comptes (2025) finds social bias "à notes et vœux équivalents".
2. **Supply, not only guidance, decides voie pro outcomes.** Only 58.1% of first wishes in voie pro are satisfied (CAP 49.9%), against 87% in GT, and 3,391 LP places were cut in 2025 (Repères 2025). Better guidance without more places in demanded specialities shifts the frustration rather than removing it.
3. **Grades decide the speciality, so a bad 3e year is costly.** The Affelnet score rests on 3e grades and socle mastery, and the DNB score strongly predicts bac success in voie pro (odds ratio up to 2.66, NI 26.24). Flexible re-orientation in 2de pro (11% do not reach 1re pro) matters as much as getting the first choice right.
4. **Redoublement is a family right, not an option the school proposes.** It appears only as *maintien* after a disagreement (2.5% of decisions, of which 17% ask for maintien). Offering a structured "année de consolidation" would need a new design, not a return to repetition, which the evidence elsewhere in this note set does not favour.
5. **Rural distance is real, about 23 km to the lycée** (INSEE 2022). Remote lycées are more often vocational with boarding (DEPP 2023). Choice of speciality, not assignment satisfaction (85%), is the constraint. Levers are boarding places, transport, and regional speciality maps planned at the *bassin* level, which the Cour calls "le niveau pertinent".
6. **Low-cost parent meetings in 3e work, but in a specific direction.** Goux, Gurgand & Maurin (2017) cut dropout by 25–40% among unrealistic planners by aligning plans with attainment. They are a strong template for the 3e dialogue, provided the dialogue also opens options (strengths, apprenticeship, re-orientation routes) rather than only lowering ambition.
7. **Mentoring is the best-evidenced lever for disadvantaged 15-year-olds.** +0.5 SD for low-SES pupils, with zero effect for others (Resnjanskij et al., Germany). Target Cordées-type resources on low-SES and rural pupils, and evaluate them: Cordées reach 185,000 pupils per year with no causal evidence yet.
8. **Girls' self-censorship responds to cheap role-model exposure.** One hour raised selective STEM enrolment from 11% to 14.5% among terminale girls (Breda et al. 2023), and girls are only 8.6% of STI2D (Cour 2025). It should be scaled in 1re and terminale, where it worked (not in 2de), and focused on careers rather than on under-representation.
9. **Give guidance an owner with time, training and a framework.** The French PP role has no timetabled hours and no training (Défenseure des droits 2025; IGÉSR 2021). PsyEN numbers have fallen to about 3,330 for secondary (AN 2025). England's model is a trained Careers Leader with protected time, 8 audited benchmarks, hubs (96% coverage) and published results (6.0/8). Finland's model is specialist counsellors and 76 timetabled hours. The English benchmark evidence is correlational (−1.1% NEET per benchmark; −8% for all 8), so a French version should come with an evaluation design.
10. **Make workplace exposure and provider encounters a legal duty, not a good intention.** England mandates 6 provider encounters from Y8, and Finland mandates 2 weeks of TET in grade 9. The OECD finds that exploring and experiencing work as a teenager predicts better adult outcomes across 8 countries (2021). France's 54 h of lycée orientation time is "indicative" and has never been measured. Cnesco found only one third of lycées held the two orientation weeks in 2018. A measured, minimum entitlement starting in 4e–3e is the gap to close.
